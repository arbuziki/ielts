## The five fixes, in priority order

### 1. Task 2: answer the question and hold one position (TR 6.5 → 8)
What keeps going wrong:
- **No position, or a position that changes.** Examples: "I don't have a firm stance" (E02, E14); no view in the introduction (E06, E08); a conclusion that contradicts the body (E01, E09, E15, E22, E25).
- **Lopsided coverage.** One side or one question gets most of the words and the other gets two sentences (E13, E16, E21, E24, E27).
- **Drift and generalisation.** Examples: answering the job market instead of education (E02); a cause of waste disposal instead of waste production (E17); unsupported claims like "many studies have shown" (E11), or facts you made up (E19).

What to do in every essay:
1. **Decode the question type** and write down what it requires:
   - opinion: one clear view
   - discuss both views + opinion: both views *and* your view
   - advantages / disadvantages
   - two-part question: both questions answered about equally
2. **State your answer in the last sentence of the introduction.** On opinion questions, "it depends" or "I don't have a firm stance" is never allowed.
3. **One idea per body paragraph**, in this order: claim → explanation → *specific* example (a named place, a number, a concrete situation) → link back to the question.
4. **Balance:** for two-part or both-views questions, each part gets at least 35% of the body.
5. **The conclusion repeats the same position.** No new ideas, no "personal preference" escape.

### 2. Task 1: develop every bullet (TA 6.6 → 8)
What keeps going wrong:
- **Bullets are stated, not developed.** One sentence or one clause per bullet (L04, L15, L18, L21, L23, L27).
- **Misreading the scenario.** "Your decision to close" when the council is only *considering* it (L03). Replying to the landlord instead of the neighbors (L32). Writing about a course instead of work experience (L40).
- **Register slips in the opening and closing.** "Hello Dear Manager" (L08), "Faithfully yours" to a friend (L10), "Hello Mr Jhonson" in a formal letter (L40), ignoring a required opening (L07).
- **Padded set phrases.** "Should I be granted this opportunity… significant asset" when nothing is being granted (L19, L23, L27, L36).

What to do in every letter:
1. Underline **who you're writing to, why, and the three bullets**. Decide the register: formal, semi-formal or informal.
2. Give **each bullet 2–3 sentences**: the point, then specific detail (what / when / how much / which), then a consequence or request. Aim for 170–190 words, not 150.
3. Use this opening and closing guide:

| Reader | Opening | Closing |
|---|---|---|
| Unknown person | Dear Sir or Madam, / To whom it may concern, | Sincerely, |
| Named official | Dear Mr. Smith, | Sincerely, / Best regards, |
| Friend | Dear John, / Hi John, | Best, / Take care, |

This is the American English convention. British *Yours faithfully* / *Yours sincerely* is also accepted, but don't mix the two systems.

4. Before writing each sentence, ask: *does this answer a bullet?* If not, cut it.

### 3. Accuracy: articles, agreement and comma splices (GRA 6.8 → 8)
GRA 8 needs **at least 70% of sentences error-free and no recurring error type**. You are usually at 50–65%. The recurring types, by how often the graders flagged them:
- **Articles** (flagged in 54 of 82 pieces), e.g. "a hit or miss", "a specific AI tools" (L40).
- **Subject–verb agreement and plurals** (21 pieces).
- **Comma splices** and over-long sentences (28 pieces).
- **Verb patterns**, e.g. *resort to taking*, *look forward to joining*, *prevent X from doing* (17 pieces).

Your sentences are often 35–50 words long. Each one is a big risk: one error makes the whole sentence count as not error-free. Mix in shorter sentences.

### 4. Spelling: proofread, don't learn new words (LR 6.7 → 7–8)
Spelling was flagged in 73 of 82 pieces. Most errors are **typos**, not words you don't know: *fowrard, thier, coprorations, memebers, imact, ultimatelly*. Others are **doubled letters**: *commited, reccuring, greately, gratitute, convinient, experinece, aquiring*.
- Reserve **3 minutes per task for a proofread**. Read once just for spelling, from the last sentence backwards, so you see words rather than meaning. The computer test has no spellcheck.
- Keep a personal list of words you misspell (see `error-log.md` below).

### 5. Vocabulary and cohesion: precise, not showy (LR and CC → 8)
- **Stock phrases earn nothing towards vocabulary range** and lose marks when they don't fit. Drop pre-learned introductions ("rapidly becoming an anachronism") and replace them with words about the topic.
- **Idioms you're not sure of** count as errors: "barely touch the surface" (should be *scratch*), "bugged down" (*bogged*), "deeply-seated" (*deep-seated*). If in doubt, use the plain collocation.
- **Linkers must match the logic.** Don't use "On the other hand" to continue the same argument (E42), and drop the Firstly / Moreover / Furthermore chains. Use *this / such / these* to refer back instead.

## The 6-week plan
Assumes about 45–60 minutes a day, 6 days a week. Stretch or compress it to fit your test date.

### Weeks 1–2: Task 2 Task Response
- **Daily, 15 min: plan only, no writing.** Take a new Task 2 prompt and write:
  - the question type
  - your position in one sentence
  - two body-paragraph claims, each with one *specific* example
  - check that every part of the question is covered
  Ten plans a week build the habit faster than five full essays.
- **3× a week: rewrite one of your own weak essays** in 40 minutes, keeping your ideas but fixing the structure. Start with E02, E06, E14, E01, E15, E24 and E27. Compare with the old version: is the position in the introduction? Are the paragraphs balanced?
- **1× a week: a full timed essay** from a new prompt. Grade it yourself with the quick rubric in `reference/ielts-calibration.md` §8.

### Week 3: Task 1 Task Achievement
- **Daily, 10 min:** for a new letter prompt, write the register, the opening and closing, and **3 specific details per bullet**, as notes only.
- **4× this week: rewrite one weak letter** in 20 minutes: L04, L15, L18, L21, L23, L27, L36, L40. Target 170–190 words, with every bullet at 2–3 sentences and no "Should I be granted…" unless something is actually being granted.
- **2× this week: a timed new letter.** Check the opening and closing against the table above.

### Week 4: Accuracy sprint
- **Make `error-log.md`.** Go through your 10 most recent feedback files and copy every correction into four sections: articles, agreement/plurals, verb patterns, spelling. Add to it after every new piece.
- **Daily, 15 min: sentence surgery.** Take one paragraph you wrote earlier and count the error-free sentences, then rewrite until 80% are error-free. Split any sentence over 35 words.
- **Article drill:** for each noun, ask *countable?* → *specific or general?* → *first mention?* Do this on one paragraph a day until it becomes automatic.
- Keep writing **one full essay and one letter** this week, and apply the proofreading routine.

### Weeks 5–6: Full timed tests
- **4× a week: a full test**, 60 minutes, Task 1 and Task 2 together, using the timings below. Use the official sample tasks on ielts.org, Cambridge IELTS General Training books, and engnovate prompts you haven't done.
- **After each test**, grade both pieces against the rubric and add new errors to the log. Rewrite the weaker piece the next day.
- **Your measure:** your last 6 essays should have a clear position, balanced parts, 70% or more error-free sentences and at most 1 spelling error. When you hit that consistently, you are at 7.5+.

- [ ] Question type identified; every part answered
- [ ] Position in the introduction, held in every paragraph, same in the conclusion
- [ ] Each body paragraph: one claim, explanation, *specific* example
- [ ] Parts balanced (each at least about 35% of the body)
- [ ] 270–320 words
- [ ] Proofread: articles, agreement, spelling, sentences over 35 words
