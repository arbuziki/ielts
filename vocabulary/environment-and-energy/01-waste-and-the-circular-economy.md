## Vocabulary

### Key terms
| Chunk | Meaning | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| a throwaway culture | a way of life in which things are thrown away quickly instead of being kept or repaired | It is **a throwaway culture**, not a lack of recycling bins, that fills our landfills with barely used goods. | C, usually singular with *a* / *the*; neutral · Grammar: cleft sentence |
| single-use plastics | plastic items made to be used once, e.g. straws, cups, bags | Were the government to ban **single-use plastics** outright, coffee shops would have to switch to reusable cups and paper straws. | plural; neutral; always hyphenate *single-use* · Grammar: inversion conditional |
| planned obsolescence | designing products so that they break or go out of date quickly | Not only does **planned obsolescence** waste resources, but it also forces consumers to replace devices that could easily be repaired. | U, no article; formal · Grammar: negative inversion |
| the circular economy | a system in which products and materials are reused, repaired, and recycled rather than thrown away | If we had embraced **the circular economy** decades ago, our landfills would not be overflowing with discarded electronics now. | with *the*; opposite: *a linear economy* · Grammar: mixed conditional |
| a landfill | a place where trash is buried in the ground | It's high time our city stopped sending recyclable materials to **a landfill** and invested in a modern sorting facility. | C in AmE: *end up in a landfill / in landfills*; BrE often U: *go to landfill* (no article) · Grammar: unreal past |
| packaging | the materials used to wrap goods | Wrapped in layers of plastic **packaging**, even a small phone charger can create a surprising amount of trash. | U: NOT *packagings*, NOT *a packaging* · Grammar: participle clause |
| household waste | trash produced by homes | The more online shopping people do, the more **household waste** they produce in the form of cardboard boxes. | U: NOT *household wastes* · Grammar: the more…, the more… |
| extended producer responsibility | a policy that makes manufacturers pay for collecting and recycling what they sell | Under **extended producer responsibility**, the cost of collecting packaging should be borne by manufacturers rather than taxpayers. | U; formal; abbreviation EPR · Grammar: modal passive |
| a deposit return system | a system in which you pay a small extra charge for a bottle and get it back when you return it | Several US states have introduced **a deposit return system**, whereby shoppers get a few cents back for every empty bottle. | C; neutral; BrE: *a deposit return scheme*; US state laws of this kind are called *bottle bills* · Grammar: relative: whose / whereby / in which |
| e-waste | thrown-away electronic devices | **E-waste** is thought to be the fastest-growing type of household trash, largely because gadgets are replaced so often. | U; also *electronic waste* · Grammar: impersonal passive |
| compost | rotted food and yard waste used to feed soil | Many households turn food scraps into **compost**, thus cutting the amount of organic waste sent to landfills. | U; verb: *to compost sth*; *yard* (AmE) = *garden* (BrE) · Grammar: result clause with thereby/thus |

## Text 1 — Problem and solution
> In many countries, the amount of trash produced by households is increasing every year. Why is this happening, and what can be done to reduce it?

The steady rise in **household waste** can largely be traced to two trends. First, cheap goods and fast delivery have created **a throwaway culture**: clothes are worn a handful of times, and gadgets are replaced long before they **wear out**, partly because of **planned obsolescence**. Second, almost everything we buy now arrives in **excessive packaging**, which alone **accounts for** a large share of what families **throw away**. Since few people ever see where their trash goes, the problem stays **out of sight, out of mind**.

Tackling this requires action **at the source** rather than simply more recycling. One idea often **put forward** is to make manufacturers responsible for their products **from cradle to grave**. Under **extended producer responsibility**, companies that **generate** the most **waste** would pay for its disposal, giving them a reason to use less material and to design goods that can be repaired. Governments can also **phase out** the most wasteful items, as the EU did with its 2019 directive on **single-use plastics**, which banned products such as plastic straws and utensils from 2021. Laws like these **bring about** change far faster than appeals to individuals. In addition, local governments should **carry out** regular checks on what residents **dispose of**, so that campaigns target the items most often thrown away. Only a genuine shift toward **the circular economy** will stop trash from **piling up** year after year.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *a throwaway culture*, *planned obsolescence* — the two standard "why" causes for any consumption question; *the circular economy* is your one-phrase solution and always takes *the*.
- **Topic vocabulary:** *extended producer responsibility* (U, no article) — the precise name for "make companies pay"; sounds expert without being ornate.
- **Collocations:** *excessive packaging*, *generate waste*, *dispose of sth* — note *dispose* needs *of*.
- **Collocations:** *reduce waste at the source* — shortened here to *action at the source*; reuse with pollution, crime, illness (*tackle the problem at the source*). BrE drops the article: *at source*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *account for* + a share / % — "X alone accounts for a large share of Y" fits any cause-analysis paragraph.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *put forward* + idea/proposal (often passive: *an idea often put forward is…*); *phase out* + noun = stop gradually; *bring about* + change; *carry out* + surveys / checks.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *wear out*, *throw away*, *pile up* — plain, accurate verbs that replace vague *get bad* / *become many*.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *out of sight, out of mind* — works for landfills, nursing homes, prisons, poverty abroad.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *as the EU did with its 2019 directive on…, which banned…* — frame for slotting in a real example: *as [country] did with [policy] in [year], which…*
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *Laws like these bring about change far faster than appeals to individuals* — reusable verdict for any "government vs. individual" question.
- **AmE spelling & usage:** *trash* (BrE *rubbish*), *utensils* (BrE *cutlery*), *toward* (BrE *towards*).

## Text 2 — Letter (formal)
> Write a letter to your city council. In your letter
> - complain that recycling bins on your street are collected irregularly and often overflow
> - explain how this affects residents
> - say what you would like the city to do.

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to complain about the irregular collection of recycling bins on Elm Street, where I have lived for the past six years.

Since the city **phased in** collections every two weeks last spring, the service has become unreliable. Pickups have been missed three times in the past two months, and on each occasion the bins overflowed within days. Bags of cardboard and plastic now **pile up** on the sidewalk, where raccoons tear them open and the wind scatters the contents. This is unsanitary, and elderly residents can no longer walk past safely.

I would like to **point out** that most households here **separate their trash** carefully. It is disheartening to see that effort **go to waste**, and some neighbors have now **opted for** the regular trash can instead, which can only lower **recycling rates**.

I would therefore be grateful if you could restore weekly pickups or provide larger bins. Could you please also **lay out** a clear schedule and inform residents whenever a pickup is canceled? If the situation does not improve, I cannot **rule out** raising the matter with my council member.

I look forward to your reply.

Sincerely,

Daniel Novak

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *recycling rates* (plural) — the measurable result the city cares about; ending a complaint with a consequence for *them* is persuasive.
- **Collocations:** *separate their trash* — *trash* is U, so no *a* and no plural; *go to waste* — no article.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *phase in* + noun = introduce gradually (*phased in collections every two weeks*); pairs naturally with *phase out* in Text 1.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *point out* + that-clause — polite, formal way to add a fact in your favor.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *opt for* + noun = choose; *lay out* + a schedule / plan / rules = present clearly (BrE also: *set out*).
- **Phrasal verbs:** *pile up* — reused from the table; vivid but neutral.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *I cannot rule out raising the matter with…* — a polite, formal threat; swap in *the mayor's office*, *my lawyer*, *the local newspaper*.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *I would therefore be grateful if you could…* / *Could you please also…?* — two different request frames so you do not repeat *I would like* (AmE: *Could you please…*, not *Please could you…*).
- **Register:** formal — *Dear Sir or Madam,* + *Sincerely,* (BrE: *Yours faithfully*). BrE equivalents you may meet in IELTS reading/listening: *fortnightly* (= every two weeks), *pavement* (= sidewalk), *councillor* (= council member), *cancelled* (= canceled).
