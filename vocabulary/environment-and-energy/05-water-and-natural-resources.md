# Water & Natural Resources
*Environment & Energy · water shortages and droughts, waste of water and energy at home, water footprints and overuse, polluted rivers and oceans, utility pricing and saving habits*

## Vocabulary

### Key terms
| Chunk | Meaning | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| a water shortage | a situation in which there is not enough water | Southern Spain is facing **a severe water shortage**. | C; also *water scarcity* (U, formal) |
| drought | a long period without rain | Years of **drought** have destroyed crops across the region. | U (the condition) / C (*a drought* = one period) |
| watering restrictions | official rules limiting when people can water lawns, wash cars, or fill pools | The city imposed **watering restrictions** after the dry summer. | plural; BrE: *a hosepipe ban* |
| a smart meter | a device that shows how much gas, electricity, or water a home is using | With **a smart meter**, you can see your electricity use jump every time the air conditioner turns on. | C; *have / install a smart meter* |
| a water footprint | the total amount of water used to produce sth or used by a person | It takes about 2,700 liters of water to make one cotton T-shirt, so fashion has a huge **water footprint**. | C; cf. *a carbon footprint*; *reduce your water footprint* |
| natural resources | materials from nature such as water, minerals, forests, and fuels | Many **natural resources** are being used faster than they can be replaced. | plural |
| utility bills | bills for water, gas, and electricity | Rising **utility bills** are squeezing household budgets. | C, plural; *utilities* = the services |
| standby | a mode in which a machine is switched on but not in use | TVs left on **standby** still use electricity. | U; *on standby* (no article) |
| a reservoir | an artificial lake that stores water for a town | **The reservoir** was only a quarter full by August. | C |
| sewage | waste water from toilets and drains | Water utilities in England have been fined for releasing **sewage** into rivers. | U: NOT *sewages* |
| a water-saving device | a fitting on a faucet, shower, or toilet that reduces the amount of water used | Installing **water-saving devices** on showers cuts water use without making them less pleasant. | C, usually plural; hyphenate *water-saving* |
| groundwater | water held underground in soil and rock | Farmers are pumping **groundwater** faster than rain can replace it. | U |

## Text 1 — Two-part question
> Many people waste water and energy at home without realizing it. Why do people do this, and how can they be encouraged to use resources more carefully?

Most people waste water and energy not because they are careless but because these resources feel unlimited. In wealthy countries water is always **on tap**, water bills often arrive only every few months, and few households have any idea how much they **go through** in a day. As a result, people **leave** lights **on** in empty rooms or **leave the water running** while brushing their teeth, usually **out of habit**. Put simply, we **take** clean water and cheap power **for granted** until they become scarce.

The most effective solution is to make consumption visible. **Smart meters**, which display in real time how much electricity a home is using, show families that small savings **add up**. Water utilities could also **charge** households **according to usage**, so that heavy users pay **the true cost of** what they consume. Campaigns work best when they **build on** practical tools like these instead of **relying on** guilt. Cape Town's crisis in 2018 was **a wake-up call**: as its **reservoirs** came close to **drying up**, the city limited residents to 50 liters (about 13 gallons) per person per day, and many households **turned to** quick showers and reusing laundry water. Governments elsewhere should **look into** metering and smart meters now, rather than waiting until they are forced to **resort to** emergency rationing. **Every drop counts**, but only when people can see where it goes.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *smart meters* (C), *reservoirs* (C) — concrete nouns that make a solution sound real rather than vague.
- **Collocations:** *leave the water running* (BrE: *leave the tap running*), *charge sb according to usage*, *the true cost of* + noun — precise phrases for the causes and the price-based solution.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *go through* + an amount; *add up* (intransitive) — natural ways to talk about quantities.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *build on* + tools / success; *rely on* + guilt / sb — here contrasted: *build on X instead of relying on Y*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *turn to* + an alternative (= start using); *resort to* + a last option (formal, negative); *look into* + measures (= investigate).
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *on tap*, *out of habit*, *take sth for granted* — three "why" explanations for any waste/consumption question; note no article in *out of habit*.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *not because…but because…* — a clear opening frame for "why" questions in any topic.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *X was a wake-up call: as…, the city…* — introduce a real example with a colon.

## Text 2 — Letter (semi-formal)
> Write a letter to the manager of the apartment building where you live. In your letter
> - report a water leak in a shared area that has not been repaired
> - explain why it is a problem
> - suggest some ways the building could save water and energy.

Dear Mr. Harris,

I am writing about a leak in the basement laundry room, which was first reported three weeks ago but has still not been repaired.

Water is dripping from a pipe near the ceiling and collecting by the door. Our janitor already works hard to **look after** the building, and it isn't fair to expect him to **mop up** a puddle every morning when the problem really needs a plumber. Someone could easily slip, and the moisture is starting to damage the wall. Besides, it is clean water going straight **down the drain**.

While the leak is being fixed, it might be worth looking at other savings. Lights in the hallways are **left on** all night, so motion sensors could **make a real difference**. **Water-saving devices** on the laundry room faucets would also help. I appreciate that you will need to **weigh** the costs, but perhaps you could **figure out** how quickly these changes would pay off. A short note in the lobby asking residents to **turn off** lights and report leaks promptly would be useful too.

I look forward to hearing from you.

Best regards,

Elena Petrova

**Notes**
- **Collocations:** *mop up a puddle*, *left on all night* — everyday chunks that describe the problem precisely.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *look after* + the building / sb (= take care of); *turn off* + lights / the heat (BrE also: *switch off*) — plain verbs for practical requests.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *figure out* + how quickly / the cost (BrE: *work out*); *weigh* + the costs / options (BrE: *weigh up*) — natural in polite suggestions.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *(go) down the drain* — literal and figurative here, which makes it memorable.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *make a real difference* — NOT *do a difference*; safe in any register.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *While the leak is being fixed, it might be worth looking at…* — a soft way to move from complaint to suggestions.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *I appreciate that you will need to…, but perhaps you could…* — polite request frame for any letter to a manager or official.
- **Register:** semi-formal — *Dear Mr. Harris,* (AmE puts a period after *Mr.*) + *Best regards,* (or *Sincerely,*), one or two contractions (*isn't*), no slang. AmE vs. BrE: *hallways* / *faucets* / *janitor* (BrE often *corridors* / *taps* / *caretaker*).
