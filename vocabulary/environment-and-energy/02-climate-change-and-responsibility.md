# Climate Change & Who Should Act
*Environment & Energy · global warming, emissions and footprints, extreme weather, and the roles of governments, companies, and individuals*

## Vocabulary

### Key terms
| Chunk | Meaning | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| climate change | long-term changes in the Earth's climate, especially warming caused by human activity | **Climate change** is already affecting harvests in southern Europe. | U, no article: NOT *the climate change* |
| global warming | the rise in the Earth's average temperature | **Global warming** is melting glaciers faster than scientists expected. | U, no article |
| carbon emissions | carbon dioxide released into the air, e.g. from burning fuel | Aviation is responsible for a growing share of **carbon emissions**. | plural; also *greenhouse gas emissions* |
| a carbon footprint | the total amount of carbon dioxide a person or organization causes | Flying less is the quickest way to shrink **your carbon footprint**. | C; *reduce / shrink / calculate your carbon footprint* |
| a carbon tax | a tax on fuels or products according to how much carbon they release | **A carbon tax** makes polluting activities more expensive. | C; *introduce / impose a carbon tax* |
| fossil fuels | coal, oil, and gas | Most of the world's energy still comes from **fossil fuels**. | C, usually plural |
| extreme weather | unusually severe heat waves, storms, floods, and droughts | **Extreme weather** is becoming more frequent and more costly. | U; C in *extreme weather events* |
| net zero | a state in which a country removes as much carbon as it emits | The UK has a legal target to reach **net zero** by 2050. | U, no article; *reach / achieve net zero* |
| greenwashing | making a company or product seem more environmentally friendly than it really is | Calling a plastic bottle "eco" because the label is recycled is pure **greenwashing**. | U; neutral; verb: *to greenwash* |
| carbon offsetting | paying for projects such as tree planting to "cancel out" your emissions | Critics say **carbon offsetting** lets airlines avoid real change. | U; noun *an offset* (C) |
| a heat wave | a period of unusually hot weather | **A heat wave** in 2022 pushed temperatures in England above 40°C (104°F) for the first time. | C; usually two words in AmE; BrE: *heatwave* |

## Text 1 — Discuss both views and give your opinion
> Some people believe that tackling climate change is mainly the responsibility of governments and large companies, while others think individuals must change their own behavior. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Those who place responsibility on governments and corporations emphasize where emissions actually come from. Most **carbon emissions** **stem from** power plants, heavy industry, and transportation networks that no ordinary citizen controls. Only states can **put a price on carbon** or **set legally binding targets**, and it was governments, not consumers, that signed the 2015 Paris Agreement, which aims to keep **global warming** well below 2°C. From this perspective, urging families to recycle more simply lets the biggest polluters **pass the buck**.

Others, however, insist that individuals cannot wait for politicians to act. Each person has **a carbon footprint**, and if millions **cut back on** flying, eat less meat, or **switch to** public transportation, the combined effect is considerable. Supporters of this view **draw on** the history of smoking: once enough people had changed their habits, governments found it far easier to **roll out** strict smoking bans. In other words, shifts in public behavior can **give rise to** tougher regulation rather than simply following it.

In my opinion, both sides are right, but their roles are different. Governments and large companies must make the low-carbon option the cheapest and easiest one, while citizens should **lead by example** and **call for** stronger policies, voting for candidates who take the issue seriously. **There is no silver bullet**, yet these two kinds of effort reinforce each other.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *carbon emissions* (plural), *global warming* (U, no article), *a carbon footprint* (C, *a*/*your*) — three nouns that need three different article choices; learn them as chunks.
- **Collocations:** *put a price on carbon*, *set legally binding targets* — exact names for what only governments can do; stronger than *make rules*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *stem from* + noun — "Most X stem from Y" is a clean cause sentence for any topic (crime, obesity, traffic).
- **Phrasal verbs:** *cut back on* + noun / -ing; *switch to* + noun; *roll out* + a ban / program — three plain, precise verbs in one sentence pattern.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *draw on* + the history / experience / evidence of… — a neat way to introduce an analogy.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *call for* + stronger policies / a ban (= demand publicly).
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *give rise to* + noun (formal; often problems, but also reforms) — a fixed verb phrase, not a phrasal verb.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *pass the buck*, *lead by example* — idiomatic but not slangy; fine in an essay.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *it was governments, not consumers, that signed the 2015 Paris Agreement, which aims to…* — a cleft sentence (*it was X, not Y, that…*) plus a real example in a relative clause.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *There is no silver bullet, yet these two kinds of effort reinforce each other.* — a compact conclusion for any "discuss both views" essay.
- **AmE usage:** *power plants* (BrE *power stations*), *public transportation* (BrE *public transport*), *behavior* (BrE *behaviour*).

## Text 2 — Essay paragraph — individual choices are largely symbolic
> Develop the opposing argument: individual lifestyle choices are largely symbolic, and only large-scale systemic change (regulation, carbon pricing, investment in infrastructure) can make a real difference.

Although it is often presented as a moral duty, shrinking your own **carbon footprint** is largely symbolic. Emissions **arise from** the way electricity is generated, goods are produced, and cities are planned, and no amount of careful shopping can change these systems. Tellingly, the idea of a personal carbon footprint was popularized in the mid-2000s by an advertising campaign for the oil company BP, which shifted attention away from producers. Focusing on individual guilt can therefore **contribute to** **greenwashing** and delay genuine reform. Policies that **put a price on carbon**, by contrast, match **the scale of the problem**. After Britain introduced a minimum carbon price in 2013, coal power quickly became uneconomic, and the country closed its last coal-fired power plant in 2024. This policy, together with the growth of wind power, **resulted in** a far greater fall in emissions than any recycling campaign could achieve. Similarly, investment in rail lines and charging stations makes the low-carbon choice the default rather than a sacrifice. Such spending saves money **in the long run**, because every year of delay **leads to** higher costs and more **extreme weather**, and only governments have the tools to **deal with** a crisis of this size. Individual effort is welcome, but at best it is **a step in the right direction**, not a substitute for policy.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *greenwashing* (U) — names the trick precisely; reuse for fashion, airlines, food labels.
- **Topic vocabulary:** *extreme weather* (U) — no article and no plural unless you add *events*.
- **Collocations:** *match the scale of the problem* — a neat way to say a solution is big enough; negative: *does not match the scale of the problem*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *arise from* + noun (formal, for problems); *contribute to* + noun (= be one of the causes) — a cause pair that avoids repeating *cause*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *result in* + a fall / rise in sth; *lead to* + higher costs — effect verbs; note *a fall in emissions* (*in*, not *of*).
- **Phrasal verbs:** *deal with* + a crisis / problem — neutral, safe in any register.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *Although it is often presented as…, X is largely symbolic.* — a strong opening for a counterargument paragraph.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *makes the [good] choice the default rather than a sacrifice* — reusable for healthy eating, public transportation, saving money.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *Individual effort is welcome, but at best it is a step in the right direction, not a substitute for policy.* — a concession-plus-verdict closing.
