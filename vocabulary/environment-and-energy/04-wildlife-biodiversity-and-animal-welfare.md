# Wildlife, Biodiversity & Animal Welfare
*Environment & Energy · endangered species, habitat loss, poaching, zoos and captive breeding, conservation, and the ethical treatment of animals*

## Vocabulary

### Key terms
| Chunk | Meaning | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| an endangered species | a type of animal or plant that may soon disappear | The blue whale is said to remain **an endangered species** decades after commercial whaling was banned. | C; *species* is the same in singular and plural: NOT *specie* · Grammar: impersonal passive |
| biodiversity | the variety of plants and animals in an area | It is tropical rainforests that have the highest **biodiversity** of any habitat on land. | U, no article; *loss of biodiversity* · Grammar: cleft sentence |
| habitat loss | the destruction of the places where animals live | Not only does **habitat loss** threaten most wild species, but it also pushes animals into conflict with farmers. | U, no article · Grammar: negative inversion |
| deforestation | cutting down large areas of forest | Driven largely by cattle ranching, **deforestation** in the Amazon releases huge amounts of stored carbon. | U, no article · Grammar: participle clause |
| poaching | illegally hunting or catching animals | **Poaching** became so widespread that rhinos were pushed close to extinction in several African countries. | U; *a poacher* (C) · Grammar: so/such… that |
| a captive breeding program | a program in which animals are bred in zoos to increase their numbers | If zoos had not started **a captive breeding program**, the Arabian oryx would no longer exist today. | C; BrE: *programme* · Grammar: mixed conditional |
| a natural habitat | the place where an animal normally lives | Even though modern zoos offer larger enclosures, elephants are far happier in their **natural habitat**. | C; *its / their natural habitat* · Grammar: concession |
| a nature preserve | protected land where wildlife can live safely | The marsh was turned into **a nature preserve** in which herons and rare frogs can breed undisturbed. | C; also *a wildlife refuge*; BrE: *a nature reserve* · Grammar: relative: whose / whereby / in which |
| a national park | a large area of land protected by the state | In 1872, the US made Yellowstone the world's first **national park**, thereby protecting its geysers and wildlife from development. | C; capitals in names: *Kruger National Park* · Grammar: result clause with thereby/thus |
| factory farming | keeping large numbers of animals indoors in small spaces to produce cheap meat | The more cheap meat people demand, the more **factory farming** spreads at the expense of animal welfare. | U; also *intensive farming* · Grammar: the more…, the more… |
| animal welfare | the health and well-being of animals | Shoppers are paying more attention to **animal welfare**, which has pushed supermarkets to stock more free-range eggs. | U, no article; *animal welfare standards* · Grammar: sentential relative |
| the illegal wildlife trade | the buying and selling of protected animals and their parts | **The illegal wildlife trade** must be tackled at both ends, by stopping poachers and reducing demand for ivory. | with *the*; formal · Grammar: modal passive |

## Text 1 — Advantages and disadvantages
> Many endangered species are now kept in zoos and wildlife parks in order to protect them. Do the advantages of this outweigh the disadvantages?

Zoos and wildlife parks can act as **a safety net** for animals that are **on the brink of extinction**. The clearest example is the California condor: by 1987 every remaining bird—just 27 in total—had been taken into **a captive breeding program**, and today there are several hundred, many of which have been **released back into the wild**. By exchanging animals with each other rather than capturing new ones, zoos have also **brought down** the number of animals taken from **their natural habitat**. They also **raise funds for** fieldwork, which is vital if conservation is to **keep up with** the speed at which **habitat loss** is happening.

That said, there are serious drawbacks. Large animals such as elephants often suffer in **cramped conditions**, and their behavior **in captivity** can differ greatly from that of their wild relatives. Keeping them is expensive: rising food and energy prices have **driven up** operating costs, forcing some zoos to **scale back** breeding work. Critics also argue that zoos **hold back** real progress by suggesting that the problem has been solved while **poaching** and **deforestation** continue elsewhere.

Overall, I believe the benefits outweigh the drawbacks, but only for zoos that concentrate on species which genuinely need help and that aim to return them to their habitats. Without such institutions, several species would already be **gone for good**.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *a captive breeding program*, *habitat loss*, *poaching*, *deforestation* — the four core terms of any wildlife essay; note that the last three are U with no article.
- **Collocations:** *cramped conditions* (plural), *raise funds for* + noun — precise ways to say "small cages" and "get money".
- **Phrasal verbs:** *bring down* + costs / numbers; *drive up* + costs / prices — an opposite pair; learn them together.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *scale back* + work / plans (formal); *hold back* + progress — both useful in any "disadvantages" paragraph.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *keep up with* + the speed / pace at which + clause — a frame for change that is happening too fast; *which is vital if X is to keep up with…* adds a condition.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *on the brink of extinction*, *in captivity* vs. *in the wild* — watch the articles: no article in *in captivity*, *the* in *in the wild*.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *The clearest example is X: in [year]…, and today…* — before-and-after example frame.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *Overall, I believe the benefits outweigh the drawbacks, but only for…* — answers the question directly and adds a condition.
- **AmE spelling:** *program* (BrE *programme*), *behavior* (BrE *behaviour*); *operating costs* is the usual AmE phrase (BrE also *running costs*).

## Text 2 — Essay paragraph — habitats, not cages
> Develop a different argument: protecting and restoring natural habitats (national parks, anti-poaching laws, limits on deforestation) is far more effective than keeping animals in captivity.

A zoo can save a handful of animals, but only a healthy habitat can support **a breeding population** large enough to survive for centuries. The mountain gorilla illustrates this well. In the early 1980s, only around 250 remained in the Virunga mountains of central Africa, yet thanks to **national parks**, **anti-poaching patrols**, and carefully controlled tourism, the population there has more than doubled to over 600. Wherever authorities **step up** patrols, **crack down on** **the illegal wildlife trade**, and **hand out** heavy fines to those caught, numbers tend to recover. Habitat protection also benefits countless other species at once: when land is **set aside** as **a nature preserve**, insects, birds, and plants are protected along with the famous animals that attract visitors. Of course, this approach means that governments must **take on** the cost of paying rangers and compensating farmers, and limits on **illegal logging** may be unpopular with powerful industries. Nevertheless, as farms and roads **take up** more and more land, the choice is stark: unless we **restore habitats** now, many animals will survive only behind glass. Zoos treat the symptoms of **habitat loss**; protecting the wild treats the cause.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *a breeding population*, *a nature preserve*, *anti-poaching patrols* — exact terms that make a conservation argument sound informed (BrE: *a nature reserve*).
- **Collocations:** *restore habitats*, *illegal logging*, *set aside land as a nature preserve* — verb + noun pairs that replace *make nature good again* or *cut trees illegally*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *step up* + patrols / efforts; *crack down on* + trade / crime; *hand out* + fines / sentences — a law-enforcement trio for crime, traffic, and pollution questions too.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *take on* + the cost / responsibility (= accept); *take up* + land / time / space (= occupy) — do not confuse them.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *X can save a handful of…, but only Y can…* — a contrast opener for any "which is more effective" paragraph.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *In the early 1980s, only around 250 remained…, yet thanks to…, the population there has more than doubled to over 600.* — the before-and-after example frame again, with *thanks to* + nouns.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *Zoos treat the symptoms; protecting the wild treats the cause.* — a memorable closing contrast (*treat the symptoms / the cause*) reusable for health, crime, poverty.
