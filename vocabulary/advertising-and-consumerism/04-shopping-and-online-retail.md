## Text 1 — Advantages and disadvantages
> Online shopping is gradually replacing traditional stores in many places. Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?

**It goes without saying that** online shopping offers enormous convenience. Customers can **compare prices**, read reviews and **place an order** **at the click of a button**, **around the clock**, while **next-day delivery** and **free returns** mean there is little risk involved. **There is little doubt that** **e-commerce** has also widened choice: someone living in a small town now has access to **a far wider range of** products than any local store could stock. **It is worth noting that** home delivery matters most for elderly and disabled people, for whom a shopping trip can be exhausting. On the other hand, the shift has hit **Main Street** hard. As **foot traffic** declines, **brick-and-mortar stores** **close down**, and some **downtown** areas are turning into **ghost towns** of **boarded-up storefronts**. A striking example is Toys "R" Us, once the largest toy retailer in the United States, which **filed for bankruptcy** in 2017 and closed all of its American stores the following year. **It could be argued that** jobs have simply moved from stores to **warehouses**, yet many of these roles are poorly paid and closely monitored. Nevertheless, **the fact remains that** consumers keep **turning to** online retailers because they save time and money. On balance, I believe the advantages outweigh the disadvantages, although local governments should help downtown areas adapt rather than allowing them to decline.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *e-commerce*, *foot traffic*, *Main Street*, *brick-and-mortar stores* — precise retail terms; *foot traffic* and *e-commerce* are uncountable.
- **Collocations:** *place an order*, *compare prices*, *next-day delivery*, *free returns* — the advantages side in four short chunks.
- **Collocations:** *boarded-up storefronts*, *file for bankruptcy* — the disadvantages side; British news reports about UK firms often say *go into administration* instead.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *close down* (stores), *turn to sth* (start using it instead).
- **Fixed expressions:** *at the click of a button*, *around the clock*, *a ghost town* — vivid but standard, not ornate.
- **Frames:** *It goes without saying that …* (an obvious point) and *There is little doubt that …* (a confident claim) — strong openers for the side you will support.
- **Frames:** *It is worth noting that …* adds a detail; *It could be argued that …, yet …* raises a counter-argument and answers it; *The fact remains that …* returns to your main point.
- **Specific example:** Toys "R" Us — filed for bankruptcy in 2017 and closed all its US stores in 2018; often linked to online competition, although heavy debt also played a role.
- **AmE vs BrE:** *Main Street / downtown* (BrE: *the high street / the town centre*); *foot traffic* (BrE: *footfall*); *store* (BrE: *shop*).

## Text 2 — Essay paragraph — Physical stores will survive by offering experiences, service and community
> Online shopping is gradually replacing traditional stores in many places. Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?

Even so, I am convinced that physical stores will survive if they offer what the internet cannot. A store that tries to compete on price alone is **fighting a losing battle**: online retailers have **low overhead**, and, **to make matters worse**, Main Street businesses face rising rents. **This is not to say that** traditional retail is doomed. Successful stores sell an experience rather than just products. Apple's stores, for instance, run free "Today at Apple" sessions on photography and coding, which give people a reason to visit even when they are not planning to buy. **What is more**, a good store offers **personal service**: customers can **try** clothes **on**, get advice from knowledgeable staff and take their purchase home the same day instead of waiting for a package and possibly **sending** it **back**. An algorithm cannot recognize a regular customer, **let alone** remember how she likes her coffee. Finally, stores can become meeting places. **Independent stores** that host book readings or repair workshops build **customer loyalty** that no discount can buy, **not to mention** the extra **foot traffic** they bring to neighboring cafés. Many also offer **in-store pickup**, combining online convenience with a visit in person. Ultimately, the future of Main Street lies not in copying online retailers but in offering **the human touch**, so the main drawback of online shopping is less serious than it first appears.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *independent stores*, *in-store pickup* — show how real stores are adapting.
- **Collocations:** *low overhead* (AmE, uncountable; BrE: *low overheads*), *personal service*, *build customer loyalty*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *try sth on* (object in the middle: *try clothes on*), *send sth back*.
- **Fixed expressions:** *fight a losing battle*, *the human touch* — a strong opening image and a strong closing phrase for the same paragraph.
- **Frames:** *This is not to say that …* limits your previous claim so that it does not sound extreme; reusable in any topic.
- **Frames:** *To make matters worse, …* adds a second problem; *What is more, …* adds a second advantage — do not mix them up.
- **Frames:** *X cannot do A, let alone B* (B is even harder) and *…, not to mention + noun* (an extra, obvious point) — both end a sentence with emphasis.
- **Specific example:** Apple's free "Today at Apple" sessions — a real example of a store selling an experience.
- **AmE:** *package* (BrE: *parcel*), *recognize*, *neighboring* (BrE: *recognise*, *neighbouring*).
