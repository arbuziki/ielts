## Text 1 — Two-part question
> People today buy far more things than they actually need. Why is this the case, and is it a positive or negative development?

Several factors explain why people today buy far more than they need. First, **fast fashion** and online shopping have made goods cheaper and easier to obtain than ever, so a new shirt can become **an impulse buy** that costs less than lunch. Second, social media encourages people to **keep up with the Joneses**: when friends post photos of **the latest gadgets** or expensive vacations, many feel they must match them, **regardless of** whether they can afford to. **Buy-now-pay-later plans** make this easy, tempting consumers to **live beyond their means** and **run up debts**. **It is often argued that** such spending is positive because it **fuels demand** and creates jobs. **While it is true that** consumption keeps stores and factories busy, I believe the overall effect is negative. **It is widely believed that** **material possessions** make us happier, yet the pleasure of a purchase rarely lasts, and the result is **a throwaway culture** in which clothes are worn a few times and then **thrown away**. The human cost is high as well: in 2013 the Rana Plaza building in Bangladesh, which housed factories producing clothes for Western brands, collapsed and killed more than 1,100 people, most of them garment workers. Everyone, **irrespective of** income, can **cut down on** unnecessary purchases and choose fewer, **long-lasting products**. Overconsumption, in short, benefits companies far more than it benefits consumers.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *fast fashion*, *a throwaway culture*, *material possessions* — the core of any "overconsumption" essay; note *fast fashion* takes no article, *a throwaway culture* does.
- **Topic vocabulary:** *an impulse buy*, *buy-now-pay-later plans* — concrete causes; better than vague "it is easy to buy things".
- **Collocations:** *fuel demand*, *the latest gadgets*, *long-lasting products*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *run up debts*, *cut down on sth*, *throw sth away* (passive here: *are thrown away*).
- **Idioms:** *keep up with the Joneses*, *live beyond your means* — change the possessive to fit the subject: *live beyond their / our means*.
- **Frames:** *regardless of whether + clause* (neutral) and *irrespective of + noun* (formal) — same meaning; use one in each half of the essay.
- **Frames:** *It is often argued that …* introduces the opposing view; *While it is true that …, I believe …* concedes and then rejects it. *It is widely believed that …* states a common opinion you are about to challenge.
- **Specific example:** the Rana Plaza collapse in Bangladesh (2013) — a well-known event that links fast fashion to its human cost.
- **AmE:** *First, … Second, …* (BrE often: *Firstly, … Secondly, …*); *vacations* (BrE: *holidays*); *stores* (BrE: *shops*).

## Text 2 — Letter (informal)
> You have recently decided to buy fewer things and get rid of what you do not use. Write a letter to a friend. In your letter: explain why you made this decision; describe what has changed in your life; invite your friend to a clothing swap you are organizing.

Hi Katya,

Sorry it's taken me so long to write! Guess what? I've decided to stop buying stuff I don't need. A couple of months ago I opened my closet and realized I'd **ended up with** about thirty tops, half of them with the tags still on. **There's no denying that** I'd **gotten carried away** with **retail therapy**, so I decided to **declutter** the whole apartment.

It's been amazing. I've **gotten rid of** three bags of clothes, **given away** tons of books and kitchen gadgets, and finally **paid off** my credit card because I'm no longer **shelling out for** things I'll never use. It turns out I can **do without** most of it – who knew **less** really **is more**?

Anyway, I'm organizing a clothing swap at my place on Saturday the 14th, starting at 2 p.m. Everyone brings a few things they no longer wear and takes home something "new" for free. Why don't you **come over** early and help me set up? Lisa might **stop by** too – I **ran into** her downtown last week and she loved the idea.

Think it over and **get back to me** by Thursday.

Talk soon,
Masha

**Notes**
- **Phrasal verbs (informal):** *end up with sth*, *get rid of sth*, *give sth away*, *pay sth off*, *shell out for sth*, *do without sth* — the natural spoken way to describe decluttering.
- **Idioms:** *get carried away (with sth)*, *less is more* — split here for emphasis: *less really is more*.
- **Topic vocabulary:** *retail therapy*, *declutter* — informal-neutral; perfect for a friendly letter, less so for an essay.
- **Invitation phrasal verbs:** *come over (to my place)*, *stop by* (= visit briefly) — friendly and natural (BrE: *come round*, *pop in*).
- **Other phrasal verbs:** *run into sb* (= meet by chance; also *bump into sb*); *get back to sb* (= reply later) — both very common in informal letters.
- **Frame:** *There's no denying that + clause* — admits something honestly; works in essays too (without the contraction: *There is no denying that …*).
- **AmE:** *gotten* (*I'd gotten carried away*, *I've gotten rid of*), *closet*, *apartment*, *tags*, *2 p.m.* (BrE: *got*, *wardrobe*, *flat*, *labels*, *2 pm*).
- **Register:** contractions, dashes, *Guess what?*, *tons of*, *Talk soon,* — all appropriate for a close friend.
