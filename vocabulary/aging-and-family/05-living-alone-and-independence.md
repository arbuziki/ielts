## Vocabulary

| loneliness | sadness because you have no company | **Loneliness** can affect people of any age. | U · neutral · NOT *lonelyness*; adj *lonely* ≠ *alone* |
| independence | freedom to look after yourself and make your own decisions | Living alone gives young adults **independence**. | U · neutral · adj *independent of / from* |
| privacy | freedom from being watched or disturbed | Many people live alone because they value their **privacy**. | U · neutral · NOT *a privacy* |

| a sense of community | a feeling of belonging to a local group | Block parties create **a sense of community**. | C, always *a* · neutral · *foster / build a sense of community* |
| a friendly visitor program | a program in which volunteers regularly visit or call lonely people | A volunteer from **a friendly visitor program** calls him twice a week. | C · neutral · also *a senior companion program*; BrE: *a befriending service / scheme* |

## Text 1 — Problem & solution
> More and more people are choosing to live alone. What problems can this cause, and how can they be solved?

Living alone offers **independence** and **privacy**, but **the growing trend** toward **single-person households** also creates serious problems.

The most obvious is **loneliness**. **It is one thing to** enjoy a quiet evening alone, **but quite another to** spend a whole weekend without speaking to anyone. **This is especially true of** older people who have lost a partner, as they may **feel cut off** from friends and services. **Little do** many young professionals **realize** how quickly their **support network** can shrink when they move to a new city for work. The second problem is financial. Covering **housing costs** on one income is difficult, and **nowhere is this more evident than** in expensive cities such as New York and San Francisco, where many single people struggle to **get by**.

These problems can, however, be addressed. Governments are beginning to **tackle loneliness** as a public health issue: in 2018 the UK appointed the world's first minister for loneliness, and in 2023 the US Surgeon General described loneliness as an epidemic. Doctors can also use **social prescribing**, referring isolated patients to clubs and volunteer groups. **As is often the case**, though, local action matters most. **Friendly visitor programs**, **cohousing** communities and simple **neighborliness** can ensure that people who live alone do not have to lead **a lonely existence**. Financial pressure could be eased by building more small, affordable apartments.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *independence*, *privacy*, *loneliness* (all U, no article) — the three key nouns for the pros and cons of living alone.
- **Topic vocabulary:** *social prescribing*, *friendly visitor programs* (BrE: *befriending services*), *cohousing* — specific solutions that sound far more convincing than "help lonely people".
- **Collocations:** *a/the growing trend toward + noun / -ing* — a neat opening for any "more and more people…" question; use *the* when it is the trend already named in the question; BrE: *towards*.
- **Collocations:** *tackle loneliness*, *feel cut off from*, *lead a lonely existence*, *housing costs* (plural).
- **Phrasal verbs:** *get by* (on one salary) — informal–neutral, fine once in an essay.
- **Frame — It is one thing to + inf, but quite another to + inf:** contrasts an easy situation with a hard one (*It is one thing to pass a test, but quite another to drive in a city*).
- **Frame — Little do + subject + realize + clause:** shows a hidden risk people ignore; formal, so use it once.
- **Frames — This is especially true of + noun; Nowhere is this more evident than in + noun:** narrow a general point down to a group or place, then give your example.
- **Frame — As is often the case (with + noun), + clause:** introduces a typical pattern before your main solution.

## Text 2 — Letter (semi-formal)
> Many older people in your area live alone and rarely leave their homes. Write a letter to the manager of your local community center. In your letter: describe the situation; suggest starting a weekly social club for older residents; offer to help organize it.

Dear Ms. Taylor,

My name is Olivia Grant, and I have lived on Mill Street for six years. I am writing about a problem in our neighborhood that is easy to overlook.

Several elderly residents on our street **live on their own** and hardly ever leave their homes. My neighbor, Mr. Evans, recently told me that he sometimes goes a week without a real conversation. **However independent** people **may be**, **feeling cut off** like this can seriously affect their **mental health**.

I would like to suggest starting a weekly social club for older residents at the **community center**. It could offer coffee, board games and occasional talks, and would help members **strike up friendships** and build **a sense of community**. **Whether or not** it becomes popular right away, I am sure it would make a real difference to our most **vulnerable residents**.

**I was wondering if you could** let us use the main hall on Wednesday afternoons. I would be happy to help organize the sessions and to **drop in on** neighbors to spread the word. **Would you mind letting** me know if you are interested? It would be wonderful to **make the most of** such a well-equipped hall.

Best regards,
Olivia Grant

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *community center*, *a sense of community* (always *a*), *vulnerable residents* — precise local-issue language for any letter to a city council or organization.
- **Collocations:** *live on their own*, *feel cut off*, *mental health* (U, no *a*), *strike up friendships*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *drop in on sb* — informal–neutral, fine in a semi-formal letter about neighbors.
- **Letter conventions (AmE):** *Dear Ms. Taylor,* (title with a period) and *Best regards,* or *Sincerely,* to close.
- **Frame — However + adj + subject + may be, + clause:** *However independent people may be, …* — concedes a point before your main argument.
- **Frame — Whether or not + clause, + clause:** shows your suggestion is worth trying in any case.
- **Frame — I was wondering if you could + inf:** a polite, softened request; perfect for semi-formal letters.
- **Frame — Would you mind + -ing?:** *Would you mind letting me know…?* — the verb after *mind* is always *-ing*.
- **Frame — make the most of + noun:** *make the most of the summer / the opportunity / your time* — a positive closing idea.
