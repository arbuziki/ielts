## Text 1 — Advantages vs disadvantages
> Many countries are raising the age at which people can retire and receive a pension. Do the advantages of this policy outweigh the disadvantages?

Many governments are **pushing back** **the retirement age** for a simple reason: **life expectancy** keeps rising, yet fewer young workers are **paying into** the system. **What** this policy offers **is** a way to keep pensions affordable without sharp tax increases. If nothing changes, the growing number of **retirees** will **put a strain on** **public finances**, **leaving** younger generations to fund pensions that they may never receive themselves. Working longer can also suit individuals, since many people in their sixties are healthy, enjoy the routine of work and bring **a wealth of experience** to their employers.

On the other hand, the policy does not affect everyone equally. **It is** people in **physically demanding jobs**, such as construction workers and nurses, **who** suffer most, as their bodies are often worn out long before 67. There is also a concern that older staff who **stay on** will occupy senior positions for longer and limit the **career prospects** of younger colleagues. In France, the decision in 2023 to raise the minimum retirement age from 62 to 64 proved **so** unpopular **that** it led to months of strikes and mass protests.

**Having considered** both sides, I believe the advantages outweigh the disadvantages, provided that the change is **phased in** gradually and manual workers can still **take early retirement**. A longer working life is a reasonable price to pay for **a sustainable pension system**.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *life expectancy* (U), *retirees* (C), *career prospects* (plural) — the core nouns of any pension question; *life expectancy* never takes *a*.
- **Collocations:** *put a strain on public finances* — reusable for healthcare, transportation or education spending; *a wealth of experience* (always *a wealth of*).
- **Collocations:** *a physically demanding job*, *take early retirement* (NOT *make*), *a sustainable pension system*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *push back sth* (delay), *pay into sth*, *stay on*, *phase sth in* — all neutral, so safe in Task 2.
- **Frame — What + subject + verb + is…:** *What this policy offers is…* — puts the main benefit at the end of the sentence for emphasis.
- **Frame — It is + focus + who / that…:** *It is people in physically demanding jobs who suffer most* — singles out the group most affected.
- **Frame — participle clause of result:** *…, leaving younger generations to fund…* — adds a consequence without a new sentence.
- **Frame — so + adj + that:** *proved so unpopular that it led to…* — perfect for a specific example.
- **Frame — Having + pp:** *Having considered both sides, I believe…* — a natural opening for a conclusion.

## Text 2 — Essay paragraph — Employers should keep and retrain older workers
> Some employers prefer to hire younger staff and encourage older employees to leave. Write a paragraph arguing that companies should actively keep and retrain older workers.

Employers who **write off** staff over fifty are making an expensive mistake. Experienced employees often have **such** detailed **institutional knowledge that** they can solve in minutes a problem that would take a newcomer days. **If it were not for** these colleagues, many young recruits would have no one to **learn the ropes** from, since practical **know-how** is best **passed on** through daily contact rather than training manuals. **In order for** older staff **to remain** productive, however, companies need to invest in **retraining** and adapt the workplace. A well-known case is BMW's plant in Dingolfing, Germany, which in 2007 redesigned one production line around older workers, introducing simple changes such as wooden floors and adjustable workbenches; productivity on that line went up rather than down. **But for** this willingness to adapt, the company might well have lost decades of skill to early retirement. Companies should also offer **flexible work arrangements** and **phased retirement** **so as not to** push loyal employees out before they are ready. Mixing generations is **a win-win situation**: younger staff gain experience, while older ones learn new digital skills. Tackling **age discrimination** is therefore a sound business decision as well as a legal duty.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *institutional knowledge* and *know-how* (both U, no *a*) — the strongest argument for keeping experienced staff in any work-related essay.
- **Topic vocabulary:** *retraining*, *phased retirement*, *age discrimination* — U nouns, so no article.
- **Collocations:** *flexible work arrangements* (plural; BrE: *flexible working*), *a win-win situation* (C).
- **Phrasal verbs:** *write sb off* (separable: *write them off*), *pass sth on* — *pass on skills / knowledge / traditions*.
- **Idiom:** *learn the ropes* — informal–neutral; acceptable once in an essay, ideal in letters.
- **Frame — such (a/an) + adj + noun + that:** *such detailed institutional knowledge that…* — with a U noun there is no *a*.
- **Frames — If it were not for + noun / But for + noun:** two formal ways to say "without X"; *But for* is shorter and more formal.
- **Frame — in order for + noun + to + inf:** *In order for older staff to remain productive…* — states a condition with its own subject.
- **Frame — so as (not) to + inf:** *so as not to push loyal employees out* — purpose; more formal than *to*.
