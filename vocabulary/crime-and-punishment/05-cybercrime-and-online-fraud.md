## Text 1 — Two-part question
> Online fraud and identity theft are becoming increasingly common. Why is this happening, and who should be responsible for protecting people from these crimes?

Online fraud is spreading mainly because so much of daily life has moved online. Banking and shopping now happen **at the click of a button**, which gives criminals millions of potential targets. **Fraudsters** can operate from abroad and are rarely caught, while **phishing** messages cost almost nothing to send. Many scams **target vulnerable groups** such as older people, who may **fall for** a scam call from someone **passing themselves off as** bank employees. Large **data breaches** make matters worse, since **personal data** stolen from one company can be used to **steal** people's **identities** years later.

In my view, the main responsibility lies with banks and tech companies, which profit from our online activity and have the expertise to **tighten security**. The EU's GDPR, which took effect in 2018, already requires firms to **comply with** strict data protection standards. Banks, too, cannot simply **stick to** the old rule that customers who approve a payment must bear the loss; they should be **held liable** for weak checks and **reimburse victims** promptly. Governments, meanwhile, must make sure that companies **abide by** the law and should **raise awareness of** common scams. Individuals should certainly **adhere to** basic guidelines, such as using **a strong password**, but a fair system must **allow for** human error, because even careful people occasionally click the wrong link.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:**
  - *fraudsters*, *phishing*, *data breaches* — precise words for the "why" part instead of repeating "criminals" and "the internet".
  - *personal data* (U) — NEVER *datas*; "data stolen from one company".
- **Collocations:**
  - *target vulnerable groups such as …* — reusable for health, poverty and advertising topics.
  - *tighten security*, *be held liable for sth*, *reimburse victims*, *bear the loss* — the vocabulary of responsibility.
- **Phrasal verbs:**
  - *fall for a scam call / a trick* and *pass themselves off as bank employees* — how scams work, in two chunks.
  - *comply with standards* / *abide by the law* / *adhere to guidelines* / *stick to the old rule* — four ways to say "follow rules", from formal to neutral.
  - *allow for human error* — "take into account"; reuse: *allow for delays*.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:**
  - *X is spreading mainly because …* — a direct answer to a "why" question.
  - *In my view, the main responsibility lies with …, which …* — a clear position for "who should be responsible" questions.
  - *X should certainly …, but a fair system must …* — concede a point, then return to your main view.

## Text 2 — Essay paragraph — individuals must protect themselves first
> Write a paragraph arguing that individuals themselves must take primary responsibility through basic digital literacy and caution, since laws and banks will always lag behind criminals.

Nevertheless, individuals themselves must take primary responsibility for their safety online, because laws and banks will always **lag behind** criminals. Regulators may take years to **iron out** the weaknesses in a law, whereas **fraudsters** can invent a new trick overnight, and they know that **the weakest link** in any system is usually a person rather than a machine. Basic **digital literacy** is therefore **the first line of defense**: recognizing **phishing** emails, turning on **two-factor authentication** and **thinking twice before** clicking unexpected links. Although the law **provides for** compensation in some cases, getting money back is slow and far from guaranteed. My grandfather nearly **fell victim to** exactly this kind of crime when a caller claiming to be from his bank tried to **con** him **out of** his savings by asking him to move them to a "safe account". He refused only because our family had agreed on a simple rule: hang up and call the bank's official number. Free library courses that **cater to** beginners would help many older people, and victims should **speak out** about what happened to them instead of staying silent out of embarrassment, so that others know what to **watch out for**. Above all, nobody should **put off** learning these skills until they have lost money; it is far wiser to **err on the side of caution**.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:**
  - *digital literacy* (U), *two-factor authentication* (U) — no articles; the core of any "what can individuals do" paragraph.
- **Collocations:**
  - *fall victim to a crime / a scam* — NOT *fall a victim of*.
  - *take primary responsibility for sth* — a precise alternative to "be responsible".
- **Phrasal verbs:**
  - *laws lag behind criminals / technology* — reuse in any technology essay.
  - *iron out weaknesses / problems* — fix remaining issues; but *close* (NOT iron out) *a loophole*.
  - *the law provides for compensation* (formal: makes it possible) and *courses that cater to beginners* (meet the needs of; BrE: *cater for*).
  - *speak out about sth* (tell people publicly) / *speak out against injustice* (criticize it); *put off learning sth* (+ -ing = postpone).
  - *con sb out of their savings* — informal–neutral, fine in a personal example.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:**
  - *X is therefore the first line of defense: …ing, …ing and …ing.* — a colon followed by three parallel -ing forms.
  - *Above all, nobody should …; it is far wiser to …* — a strong final recommendation.
