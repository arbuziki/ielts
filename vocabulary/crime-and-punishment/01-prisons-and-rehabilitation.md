# Prisons vs Rehabilitation
*Crime & Punishment · the purpose of prison, reoffending, overcrowding, education and training behind bars, and life after release*

## Vocabulary

### Key terms
| Chunk | Meaning | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| a custodial sentence | a punishment that involves time in prison | Judges should pass **a custodial sentence** only for serious crimes. | C · formal, mainly BrE legal term · opposite: *a non-custodial sentence*; a judge *passes* it · everyday AmE: *a prison sentence* / *jail time* |
| rehabilitation | helping offenders return to a normal, law-abiding life | Prisons should focus on **rehabilitation**, not just punishment. | U · neutral · NOT *a rehabilitation* |
| deterrence | stopping crime by making people afraid of punishment | Long sentences are supposed to work through **deterrence**. | U · formal · cf. *a deterrent* (C) = the thing that deters |
| reoffending | committing another crime after being punished | Cutting **reoffending** saves money and protects the public. | U · neutral · formal synonym, very common in AmE: *recidivism* (U) |
| a repeat offender | a person who commits crimes again and again | Most burglaries are committed by **repeat offenders**. | C · neutral · = *a persistent offender* |
| an ex-offender | someone who has completed a punishment for a crime | Few employers are willing to hire **an ex-offender**. | C · neutral · *an* before a vowel sound · AmE also: *a formerly incarcerated person* |
| an inmate | a person kept in a prison | Every **inmate** should have access to education. | C · neutral · = *a prisoner* |
| the prison population | the total number of people in prison in a country | In many countries, **the prison population** has grown faster than the number of cells. | singular, always with *the* · neutral |
| overcrowding | having too many people in too small a space | **Overcrowding** makes rehabilitation almost impossible. | U · neutral · *prison overcrowding*; NOT *an overcrowding* |
| vocational training | training in practical skills for a particular job | **Vocational training** in plumbing or cooking helps prisoners find work. | U · neutral · NOT *a vocational training* |
| a criminal record | an official record of the crimes a person has committed | Having **a criminal record** makes it hard to find a job. | C · neutral · *have / get a criminal record* |
| parole | early release from prison on condition of good behavior | He was released **on parole** after serving half his sentence. | U · neutral · *on parole*, NOT *in parole* |
| a life sentence | the punishment of staying in prison for life or a very long time | The murderer received **a life sentence**. | C · neutral · *receive / be given a life sentence* |

| Chunk | Meaning | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| serve a sentence | spend the time in prison that a court has ordered | She is **serving a** six-year **sentence** for fraud. | verb + noun · neutral · the judge *passes* (AmE also *hands down*) a sentence, the offender *serves* it |

## Text 1 — Discuss both views + opinion
> Some people believe that the best way to reduce crime is to give longer prison sentences. Others, however, think there are better ways of reducing crime. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

Supporters of longer sentences argue that keeping criminals **behind bars** protects the public and **acts as a deterrent** to anyone tempted to break the law. Politicians who want to look **tough on crime** promise **harsh sentences**, and it is true that a burglar who is **serving a** ten-year **sentence** cannot break into anyone's home.

However, I believe that longer terms do little to reduce crime in the long run. Almost every prisoner is eventually **released from prison**, and without help many **fall back into** their old habits, which turns the system into **a revolving door**. The United States **locks up** a larger share of its population than almost any other country, yet its murder rate remains several times higher than that of most Western European states. A better approach is to **set aside** money for **rehabilitation**, especially literacy classes and **vocational training**. Such programs cost money at first, but they **pay off**: each **ex-offender** who finds work is one fewer person **reoffending** **at the taxpayer's expense**. Long sentences also **pass** the problem **on** to the next generation. Children **brought up** by a parent who is in and out of jail often follow the same path, whereas a parent who has learned a trade can **hand down** useful skills.

On balance, long sentences may be justified for violent offenders, but for most crimes, investing in people is the smarter choice.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:**
  - *rehabilitation*, *reoffending* — both uncountable: "focus on rehabilitation", NOT "a rehabilitation".
  - *a revolving door* — a vivid but neutral image for people who keep returning; also works for hospitals or short-term jobs.
- **Collocations:**
  - *serve a ten-year sentence* — the length goes between *a* and *sentence*, hyphenated, no plural *-s* (*ten-year*, NOT *ten-years*).
  - *act as a deterrent to sb* — keep the article: *deterrent* is countable.
- **Phrasal verbs:**
  - *set aside money for sth* — reuse: *set aside land for housing*, *set aside time for studying*.
  - *sth pays off* — intransitive: "the investment pays off", NOT "pays off it".
  - *pass the problem on to sb* (transfer) vs *hand down skills (to children)* (from one generation to the next).
  - *children brought up by sb* — a reduced passive clause; reuse with any topic (*children brought up in cities*). AmE also says *raised by sb*.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:**
  - *X locks up a larger share of its population than almost any other country, yet …* — "comparison + yet" frame for using evidence against a view.
  - *each X who … is one fewer Y …* — a compact cost-benefit frame (*each cyclist is one fewer car on the road*).
  - *On balance, X may be justified for …, but for most …, Y is the smarter choice.* — a balanced conclusion.

## Text 2 — Essay paragraph — education and support inside prisons break the cycle of reoffending
> Write a paragraph arguing that education, vocational training and mental health support inside prisons are the most effective way to break the cycle of reoffending.

The most effective way to **break the cycle of reoffending** is to treat time in prison as an opportunity to learn rather than simply a punishment. A large proportion of **inmates** **dropped out of** school early, and many struggle with addiction or untreated mental illness, so **locking** them **up** without help does nothing to **address the root causes of** their offending. By contrast, inmates who **earn certificates**, **take up** a trade through **vocational training** and receive regular **mental health support** leave with a realistic chance of **turning their lives around**. Norway **stands out** as an example: prisons such as Halden are designed to resemble ordinary life as closely as possible, with workshops and classrooms, and the country has one of the lowest **reoffending** rates in Europe. Critics dismiss this model as soft, but the evidence **backs up** the opposite view, since **ex-offenders** with steady jobs are far less likely to commit new crimes. Politicians who **stand up for** such programs risk being accused of rewarding criminals, yet they are in fact protecting future victims. Education should therefore be a standard part of every sentence rather than an optional extra that prisoners can **opt out of**, because every former inmate who manages to **go straight** is one less burden on society.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:**
  - *inmates* (C) — a useful synonym to avoid repeating *prisoners*.
  - *vocational training* (U) — no article; pair it with *take up a trade through …*.
  - *a realistic chance of turning your life around* — *a chance of + -ing* is the safest pattern.
- **Collocations:**
  - *address the root causes of sth* — reusable for any social problem (*the root causes of obesity / homelessness*).
  - *earn certificates*, *receive mental health support* — natural verb partners; NOT *get a support*. BrE: *gain qualifications*.
- **Phrasal verbs:**
  - *drop out of school* (past: *dropped out of*) and *opt out of a program / an extra* — keep *of*.
  - *X stands out as an example* — an elegant way to introduce a real-world example.
  - *the evidence backs up the view that …* / *stand up for programs / rights* — useful for arguing.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:**
  - *The most effective way to X is to Y rather than Z.* — a strong topic sentence for any solution paragraph.
  - *Critics dismiss this as …, but the evidence backs up the opposite view, since …* — a ready-made rebuttal frame.
