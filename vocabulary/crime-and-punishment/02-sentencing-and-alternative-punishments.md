## Text 1 — Opinion (agree / disagree)
> Some people think that people who commit minor crimes should be made to do unpaid community work instead of being sent to prison. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

I largely agree that people who **commit** minor **offenses**, such as **vandalism** or petty theft, should **carry out unpaid work** rather than be sent to prison.

First, short prison terms **use up** cell space and staff time that should be reserved for violent criminals who genuinely **pose a threat to** the public. Locking up **a first-time offender** for a few weeks can also do lasting damage: they may lose their job, and families sometimes **break down** while a parent is away. Heavy **fines** are hardly a better answer, since a penalty that **wipes out** the savings of a poor household barely affects a wealthy one.

**Community service**, by contrast, allows offenders to **repair the harm** they have caused and to **face up to** its effect on real people. Britain's Community Payback program, in which offenders in high-visibility vests **clean up** graffiti and litter, is often **singled out** as a sensible model precisely because local residents can see justice being done. Spending Saturdays scrubbing walls in your own neighborhood is more likely to make you **learn your lesson** than sitting in a cell with experienced criminals.

That said, judges should decide **on a case-by-case basis**, and offenders who fail to show up must expect stricter penalties. Overall, the belief that only prison counts as real punishment is slowly **dying out**, and rightly so, because **the punishment should fit the crime**.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:**
  - *vandalism* (U), *a first-time offender* (C) — precise words instead of the vague "bad people".
  - *community service* (U) — no article: "do community service".
- **Collocations:**
  - *commit minor offenses* — the adjective goes in the middle; NEVER *make / do a crime*. BrE spelling: *offences*.
  - *pose a threat to the public*, *repair the harm* — formal, exam-friendly verb + noun pairs.
- **Phrasal verbs:**
  - *use up cell space / resources* — reuse for money, energy, natural resources.
  - *families break down* (intransitive) — also *talks break down*; transitive: *break down barriers*.
  - *wipe out sb's savings* — strong verb for total loss; reuse with *species* or *diseases*.
  - *be singled out as a model / for criticism* — great for introducing an example.
  - *a belief / tradition is dying out* — slow disappearance.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:**
  - *I largely agree that … rather than …* — a clear but nuanced opening position.
  - *X is more likely to … than …* — a simple comparative frame for any argument.
  - *Overall, …, and rightly so, because …* — a confident conclusion.

## Text 2 — Essay paragraph — community service is a soft option for repeat offenders
> Write a paragraph arguing that community service is often seen as a soft option that fails to deter, and that some minor but repeated offenses still deserve short prison sentences.

On the other hand, there is a strong case that **community service** is too often **a soft option** that fails to deter. For many offenders, a few weekends of painting fences is little more than **a slap on the wrist**, and those who see it this way are unlikely to change. The problem is most serious with persistent offenders, who **make up** a small proportion of criminals but commit a disproportionate share of thefts and burglaries. Someone who has been **let off** with unpaid work four or five times has already **thrown away** several chances, and offering the same penalty again simply teaches them that they can **get away with** almost anything. Victims matter too: a small business owner whose store is targeted by thieves every month cannot easily **make up for** the losses, and when courts **give in to** pressure to keep people out of prison, public trust in the justice system suffers. In such cases, a short prison term is not about **an eye for an eye**; it shows that the courts are prepared to **come down hard on** repeated offending. Prison should be **a last resort**, but it must stay available for offenders who refuse to **give up** crime despite every opportunity to do so.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:**
  - *persistent offenders* — a precise synonym for *repeat offenders*.
- **Collocations:**
  - *a disproportionate share of sth* — reusable with any statistic-style claim (*a disproportionate share of the cost*).
  - *public trust in the justice system* — *trust in*, NOT *trust to*.
- **Phrasal verbs:**
  - *make up a small proportion of sth* (form a part) vs *make up for the losses* (compensate) — do not confuse them.
  - *throw away a chance / an opportunity* — waste it completely.
  - *give in to pressure* / *give up crime* — *give in to* = stop resisting; *give up* = stop doing.
  - *let sb off with sth*, *get away with sth*, *come down hard on sb* — the core vocabulary of leniency vs strictness.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:**
  - *On the other hand, there is a strong case that …* — a formal way to introduce the opposing view.
  - *X is little more than a slap on the wrist* — a reusable criticism of weak penalties or fines.
  - *X should be a last resort, but it must stay available for …* — a balanced closing sentence.
