# Remote & Flexible Work
*Work & Careers · working from home, hybrid work, flexible hours, saving commuting time, isolation and blurred boundaries, team communication, the impact on downtown areas and offices*

## Text 1 — Advantages vs disadvantages
> More and more people are working from home rather than in an office. Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?

When the pandemic closed offices in 2020, millions of people began **working from home** overnight. The share of working days spent at home has fallen since then, but it has **leveled off** far above its pre-pandemic level, so the change looks permanent.

The benefits are considerable. Employees **save hours of commuting time** each week and can **work around** school pickups and other commitments. Remote work also **opens up** jobs to parents, disabled people and those in rural areas, while employers can **tap into a wider talent pool** and **cut office costs**.

Nevertheless, the drawbacks are real. Many people suffer from **a sense of isolation**, and home working can **blur the boundaries between work and home**, so that stress **builds up** unnoticed. Junior staff lose the **chance encounters** through which they learn from experienced colleagues, and managers may favor those they see every day, a tendency known as **proximity bias**.

On balance, I believe the advantages outweigh the disadvantages, as long as companies **adopt a hybrid model** rather than keep staff at home full-time. Firms should **draw up** clear rules about when people are expected to be online, and those who miss having colleagues around could **sign up for** part-time membership of **a coworking space**.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *working from home* (U, no article), *proximity bias*, *a coworking space* (C) — precise terms that replace vague phrases like *the home format*.
- **Collocations:** *save hours of commuting time*, *tap into a wider talent pool*, *cut office costs* — three advantages in three chunks, one for workers and two for employers.
- **Collocations:** *a sense of isolation*, *blur the boundaries between work and home* — the two main disadvantages; *blur the boundaries between X and Y* works for many topics (fact and opinion, news and advertising).
- **Phrasal verbs:** *level off* (= stop rising or falling) — ideal for describing a trend; *leveled off* (AmE single *l*; BrE *levelled*).
- **Phrasal verbs:** *open up* + jobs / opportunities / markets *to* + group; *stress builds up*; *build up skills / savings*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *draw up* + rules / a plan / a contract; *sign up for* + a course / a service / a program.
- **Frame:** *…, a tendency known as X* — introduces a technical term without a new sentence.
- **Frame:** *On balance, I believe… as long as…* — a conclusion with a condition, which reads as a mature judgment.

## Text 2 — Letter (semi-formal)
> You would like to work from home two days a week. Write a letter to your manager. In your letter: explain why you are making this request; describe how you would organize your work; reassure your manager that it would not affect the team.

Dear Mark,

I am writing to ask whether I could work from home on Tuesdays and Thursdays.

Since I moved to Halton, commuting to and from work has taken me almost three hours a day. In addition, since I **took over** the monthly reporting in March, most of my work has involved data analysis, which I can do far more efficiently in a quiet **home office**.

On those days I would **keep regular hours** and be online throughout our **core hours**. I would schedule client meetings for my office days, **dial into** the Monday planning session as usual and **check in** with you at the end of each home-working day. Colleagues could **reach out to** me by phone or chat at any time.

I know the team has only recently **bounced back** from last year's staff shortages, so I would suggest a three-month trial. If my work **fell short of** your expectations, I would of course return to the office full-time. Arrangements like this also help companies **hold on to** experienced staff.

I would be happy to discuss this whenever it is convenient for you.

Best regards,
Anna

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *a home office*, *core hours* (plural) — show that you have thought about the practical side.
- **Collocations:** *keep regular hours* — reassures a manager that you will be available.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *dial into* + a meeting; *check in with sb* — the language of remote team communication.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *take over* + duties / a role / a company; *hold on to* + staff / jobs / traditions.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *bounce back (from + setback)* — recovery of a team, an economy or a person after illness.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *reach out to sb* (= contact / offer help to) — neutral, very common in AmE workplace English.
- **Fixed expressions:** *fall short of* + expectations / targets (formal); in a conditional: *If my work fell short of…, I would…*.
- **Frames:** *I am writing to ask whether I could…*, *I would suggest a three-month trial*, *whenever it is convenient for you* — polite but not stiff, right for a letter to your own manager; *Dear + first name → Best regards,* (BrE: *Kind regards*).
