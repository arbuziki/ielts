# Youth Unemployment
*Work & Careers · high school and college graduates without jobs, lack of experience, the education–job market mismatch, internships and apprenticeships, government programs, long-term effects*

## Text 1 — Problem and solution
> In many countries, unemployment among young people is high. What are the causes of this problem, and what can governments do to tackle it?

Young people are the last to be **taken on** when an economy grows and the first to be **laid off** when it shrinks. At the height of the eurozone crisis in 2013, more than half of Spaniards under 25 who wanted a job were **out of work**. The root of the problem is **a catch-22**: employers want **work experience**, but **high school graduates** cannot gain it without a first job, and many firms are **put off** by the cost of training them. There is also **a skills mismatch**, as universities produce **college graduates** in crowded fields while employers struggle to **fill** technical **positions**.

Governments can respond in two ways. First, they should expand **apprenticeships**, which let young people **earn while they learn**. Germany's dual system, which combines classroom study with **on-the-job training**, is widely credited with keeping its **youth unemployment rate** among the lowest in Europe. Second, they should offer **tax breaks** to firms that hire workers under 25 and guarantee every young person a place in work or training, as the EU's Youth Guarantee has promised since 2013. Such programs need to **allow for** the time newcomers take to become productive and **cater to** rural areas, where jobs are scarce. Otherwise, **long-term unemployment** may create **a lost generation** unable to **provide for** their families, so youth organizations are right to **speak out against** cuts to training budgets.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *work experience* (U, never *a work experience*), *high school graduates*, *college graduates*, *apprenticeships* (C) — the four nouns any youth-jobs essay needs; BrE: *school leavers*.
- **Topic vocabulary:** *a skills mismatch*, *long-term unemployment* (U), *a lost generation* — cause, effect and the "big picture" consequence.
- **Collocations:** *fill positions*, *on-the-job training*, *offer tax breaks to firms* — ready-made solutions you can reuse for skills shortages in any sector.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *take sb on / lay sb off* — a neat contrast pair; passive *be taken on / be laid off* sounds natural.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *put sb off (+ by + noun)* — *firms are put off by the cost*; *put sth off + -ing* = postpone.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *allow for sth* (= take into account), *cater to* + needs / groups / areas (BrE: *cater for*) — both formal enough for Task 2.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *provide for* + a family / your children; *speak out against* + cuts / a policy — good for a closing sentence about society.
- **Fixed expressions:** *a catch-22*, *earn while you learn* — short, vivid and still neutral.
- **Frame:** *The root of the problem is X: …* — introduces the main cause in any problem–solution essay.
- **Frame:** *X is widely credited with + -ing* — presents a well-known example without inventing statistics.

## Text 2 — Letter (formal)
> You would like to gain some work experience in a company in your area. Write a letter to the company's human resources manager. In your letter: introduce yourself; explain why you are interested in the company; ask about possible internship or work-experience opportunities.

Dear Ms. Harris,

I am writing to inquire whether Northfield Logistics offers **internships** or short **job-shadowing** opportunities for students.

I am a second-year business student at Riverside College, and I am eager to **gain hands-on experience** in supply-chain management before I **enter the labor market** next year. Last summer I volunteered at a food bank, where I helped to **iron out** problems with the delivery schedule, and I discovered how much I enjoy this kind of work.

Your company interests me because of its reputation for careful planning. I read in the local newspaper how your team **headed off** a crisis during last winter's snowstorms by rerouting trucks before delays could **spill over into** the rest of the network. I would value the chance to **learn on the job** from such professionals.

I do not wish to **play down** my **lack of relevant experience**, but I learn quickly and would follow any procedures **laid down** for trainees. I am available for up to eight weeks starting in June.

I would be grateful if you could let me know whether any opportunities are available. I have attached my résumé for your consideration.

Sincerely,
Daniel Ivanov

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *internships* (C), *job shadowing* (U) — the standard names for short student work experience; *a job-shadowing opportunity*.
- **Collocations:** *gain hands-on experience*, *enter the labor market* — formal and precise; better than *get some practice*.
- **Collocations:** *a lack of relevant experience* — honest but professional; NOT *lack in experience*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *iron out problems* (= solve small difficulties), *head off a crisis* (= prevent it) — strong verbs for describing achievements in any job letter.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *spill over into* + other areas — describes knock-on problems at work, at home or in the economy.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *play down* + a weakness / the risks; *procedures / rules laid down for* + group (passive is very natural).
- **Fixed expressions:** *learn on the job* — shows willingness to learn without sounding desperate.
- **Frames:** *I am writing to inquire whether…*, *I would be grateful if you could…*, *I have attached my résumé for your consideration* — standard formal-letter openers and closers; *Dear Ms. + last name → Sincerely,* (BrE: *enquire*, *CV*, *Yours sincerely*).
