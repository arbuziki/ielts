## Text 1 — Discuss both views
> Some people believe that public transportation should be free for everyone and paid for through taxes. Others think passengers should pay for the services they use. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

Supporters of **fare-free public transit** argue that buses and trains should be treated like libraries: **free at the point of service** and **funded through taxation**. Their strongest point is fairness. **Fares** take a much larger share of a janitor's wages than a lawyer's, so removing them **opens up** jobs and training to people who would otherwise stay at home. Luxembourg, which abolished fares on buses, light rail and trains nationwide in 2020, shows that the idea can work on a national scale. Opponents reply that **there is no such thing as a free lunch**. Ticket sales help **cover the cost of** running the network, and without them transit agencies depend entirely on **the taxpayer**; in difficult years, investment is the first thing to go and a backlog of repairs **builds up**. Critics also note that the early rise in **ridership** often **levels off**, and that many new passengers are former walkers and cyclists rather than drivers. In my view, passengers should still pay, but far less than they do now. Governments should **draw up** long-term plans for cheap **transit passes** and **reduced fares** for students, seniors and job seekers, so that anyone who **signs up for** a monthly pass gets **good value for the money**, while fares continue to fund **reliable service**.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:**
  - *fares* (C, plural here) vs. *public transportation* (U) — never "a public transportation".
  - *ridership* (U) — a precise word for "how many people use it"; very common in American news.
- **Collocations:**
  - *free at the point of service / funded through taxation* — a ready-made pair for any "should it be free?" question (health care, college, museums).
  - *cover the cost of + -ing*; *get good value for the money*; *reliable service* (no article in AmE).
- **Phrasal verbs:**
  - *open up + jobs / opportunities + to + people*; *a backlog of repairs builds up*.
  - *the rise levels off* (intransitive) — describe trends without numbers.
  - *draw up + plans / a list*; *sign up for + a pass / a course / a program*.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:**
  - *there is no such thing as a free lunch* — use once, to introduce the opposing view; never change the wording.
  - *X take a much larger share of A's wages than B's* — a neat frame for any fairness argument about prices or taxes.

## Text 2 — Letter (formal)
> The bus company in your town has recently cut the number of buses on the route you use every day. Write a letter to the company. In your letter: explain how you use the service; describe the problems the changes have caused; say what you would like the company to do.

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to express my concern about the recent reduction in service on Route 12 between Riverside and downtown.

I have taken this **bus route** to work every weekday for six years, and since your company **took over** last spring, it has generally provided **reliable service**. However, since the new **schedule** took effect on September 1, buses have run every forty minutes instead of every fifteen. **Overcrowding** is now a serious problem: buses are often too full to **pick up** passengers at my stop, and I have been late for work twice. Elderly residents, for whom this bus is **a lifeline**, have been **left stranded** in the cold.

The service now **falls** well **short of** what passengers can expect. Ridership has only just **bounced back** after the pandemic, and further **service cuts** risk driving people back into their cars.

I would therefore ask you to restore the rush-hour buses and to **reach out to** regular passengers before making further changes. If you wish to **hold on to** loyal customers, I believe this is essential.

I look forward to your reply.

Sincerely,
Anna Petrova

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:**
  - *the new schedule took effect on + date* — the formal way to say "started"; AmE date order: *September 1* (BrE: *1 September*).
  - *a lifeline for + group* — shows real impact in a complaint letter.
- **Collocations:**
  - *reliable service; service cuts; pick up passengers* — precise, not "the bus didn't take people".
- **Phrasal verbs:**
  - *take over + the route / company*; *bounce back after + crisis*.
  - *fall (well) short of + what people expect / targets* — the adverb goes between *fall* and *short*.
  - *reach out to + passengers / communities*; *hold on to + customers*.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:**
  - *I am writing to express my concern about…* / *I would therefore ask you to…* / *I look forward to your reply.* — a complete formal skeleton (no contractions).
  - *If you wish to + verb, I believe this is essential.* — a polite but firm closing push.
  - *Dear Sir or Madam, … Sincerely,* — the standard AmE formal frame (BrE: Yours faithfully).
