# Air Travel & Tourism
*Transportation & Cities · budget airlines, mass tourism, the carbon footprint of flying, frequent-flyer taxes, high-speed rail, the impact of tourists on local communities*

## Vocabulary

### Key terms
| Chunk | Meaning | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| a budget airline | an airline that sells cheap tickets with few extras | **Budget airlines** made weekend trips abroad affordable. | C · neutral · formal: a low-cost carrier |
| a short-haul flight | a flight over a short distance | France has banned some domestic **short-haul flights** where a quick train alternative exists. | C · neutral · opposite: a long-haul flight |
| a carbon footprint | the amount of CO2 a person or activity produces | Flying is the quickest way to increase your **carbon footprint**. | C · neutral · reduce / shrink your carbon footprint |
| carbon emissions | CO2 released into the air | Aviation's **carbon emissions** are hard to cut. | plural · neutral |
| mass tourism | travel by very large numbers of people | **Mass tourism** has transformed the Spanish coast. | U · neutral · NOT a mass tourism |
| overtourism | too many tourists in one place | Venice is the best-known example of **overtourism**. | U · neutral (a fairly new word) |
| a frequent-flyer levy | a tax that rises with the number of flights a person takes | **A frequent-flyer levy** would target the small group who fly most. | C · formal · a levy = a tax · also spelled: frequent-flier |
| a tourist trap | a place full of overpriced attractions for visitors | The old harbor has turned into **a tourist trap**. | C · neutral, negative |
| a day-tripper | a visitor who comes for one day and does not stay the night | Venice now charges **day-trippers** an entry fee on busy days. | C · neutral |
| a getaway | a short vacation, often in a city | Cheap flights turned weekend **getaways** abroad into a habit. | C · neutral, informal · a weekend / city getaway · BrE: a city break |
| high-speed rail | very fast trains and the lines they run on | **High-speed rail** is a real alternative to flying within Europe. | U · neutral · a high-speed line / train (C) |
| the host community | the local people who live where tourists visit | Tourism must bring benefits to **the host community**. | C, with "the" · formal |

## Text 1 — Advantages and disadvantages
> Cheap flights have made international travel affordable for millions of people. Do the advantages of this outweigh the disadvantages?

Since low-cost flying **took off** in the 1990s, millions of people who once had to **settle for** a vacation close to home have been able to **broaden their horizons** abroad **at a fraction of the cost**, and many regional economies now **count on** the visitors that **budget airlines** bring. Nevertheless, I believe these benefits are now outweighed by the drawbacks. The most serious is **the environmental cost**. Aviation is one of the hardest sectors to decarbonize, yet **rock-bottom fares** encourage people to **jet off** for a weekend without a thought for their **carbon footprint**. The second problem is **overtourism**. When tourists **flock to** the same handful of cities, local residents **bear the brunt of** it: in parts of Barcelona and Amsterdam, family homes and neighborhood stores have **given way to** souvenir shops and vacation rentals. It would be unfair to **dwell on** these problems without suggesting solutions, though. France has banned **short-haul flights** on domestic routes where a train trip of under two and a half hours exists, and **a frequent-flyer levy** could make those who fly most pay for the damage they cause. When travelers **narrow down** their options, price is usually the deciding factor, so until tickets reflect their true cost, cheap flights will do more harm than good.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:**
  - *budget airlines / short-haul flights* (C, plural) vs. *overtourism* (U) — no article with the uncountable noun.
  - *a frequent-flyer levy* — a precise policy term; "levy" sounds more expert than "tax".
- **Collocations:**
  - *the environmental cost (of + -ing)*; *rock-bottom fares*; *without a thought for + noun*.
- **Phrasal verbs:**
  - *settle for + a vacation close to home / less*; *count on + visitors / sb to do sth*.
  - *jet off; flock to + place* — vivid but neutral enough for an essay.
  - *A gives way to B* (= A is replaced by B) — perfect for describing change in cities, jobs, traditions.
  - *dwell on + problems*; *narrow down + options*.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:**
  - *bear the brunt of + noun* — reuse for any group that suffers most (the poor, small businesses).
  - *until X reflects its true cost, Y will do more harm than good* — a strong conclusion frame for any "cheap but harmful" product.

## Text 2 — Letter (informal)
> You and a friend are planning a trip to another country next summer. Your friend wants to fly, but you would prefer to go by train. Write a letter to your friend. In your letter: explain why you prefer to go by train; describe the route; suggest what you could do on the way.

Hi Tom,

Thanks for your message—I can't wait for Barcelona! **It's been ages since** we went away together, so I really want this trip to be special.

I know you'd rather fly, but hear me out. Yes, the train takes longer, but **by and large** it's far more relaxing: no security lines, plenty of legroom and great views. It's also a far greener **means of transportation**, and I've been trying to shrink my **carbon footprint** this year.

Here's the plan: we **set off** from London on the Eurostar, change trains in Paris and catch the direct high-speed train to Barcelona. If we **stop off** in Paris for a night, we can **meet up** with Claire, who's living there now. We could **hang out** by the canal and **take in** the sights before heading south the next morning. It'd be a Paris **getaway** and a scenic trip in one—**the best of both worlds**!

Do you want to **get together** next weekend to look at tickets? They're much cheaper if we **book in advance**.

Talk soon,
Alex

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:**
  - *a means of transportation* — same form in singular and plural; never "a transportation mean".
  - *a getaway* (C) — natural AmE for a short vacation (BrE: a city break).
- **Collocations:**
  - *shrink my carbon footprint*; *book in advance*; *no security lines* (BrE: queues) — everyday but precise.
- **Phrasal verbs:**
  - *set off from + place*; *stop off in + place for a night*; *take in the sights* — the "trip" set for any travel letter.
  - *meet up with + sb*; *hang out + by / at + place*; *get together + time* — informal social verbs; keep them for letters to friends.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:**
  - *It's been ages since + past simple* — a warm opening for any informal letter.
  - *I know you'd rather…, but hear me out.* / *Yes, X…, but by and large…* — a friendly way to disagree, admit a drawback and then argue.
  - *Do you want to + verb…?* — the natural AmE way to suggest a plan (BrE: Shall we…?).
  - *Hi Tom, … Talk soon,* — informal AmE frame; also *Best,* / *Take care,*.
