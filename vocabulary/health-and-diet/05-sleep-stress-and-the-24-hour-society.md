## Text 1 — Advantages vs disadvantages
> Stores, services and workplaces increasingly operate 24 hours a day. Do the advantages of this development outweigh the disadvantages?

The move toward **a 24-hour society** brings genuine benefits. Hospitals and emergency services must operate **around the clock**, and late-night stores serve people who themselves **work odd hours**. Businesses that stay open longer also **take on** extra staff, creating jobs for students and parents who prefer evening work. **Even so**, I believe the disadvantages clearly outweigh these gains.

The main cost is to health. **Shift work** upsets **the body clock**, and people on **night shifts** can go weeks without **a good night's sleep**. **A chronic lack of sleep** **impairs concentration**, so tired employees **are prone to errors** and, in the worst cases, drivers **fall asleep at the wheel**. Nor is the problem limited to night workers: **an always-on culture** means that office staff **check work emails** **at all hours** and never fully **switch off**, so many eventually **burn out**. France has responded with a law on **the right to disconnect**, which since 2017 has required larger companies to agree on rules for contacting staff outside working hours. Other governments should **step up** efforts in the same direction, **crack down on** employers who ignore legal limits on working time and **hand out** heavy fines where necessary. Some supermarkets, meanwhile, have already **scaled back** 24-hour service because so few customers shop **in the early hours**. In my view, around-the-clock services should be reserved for emergencies rather than offered for convenience.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *shift work*, *a 24-hour society*, *an always-on culture* — note *an* before *always* (vowel sound).
- **Collocations:** *work odd hours* (BrE *unsocial hours*), *impair concentration* (formal), *be prone to errors*, *check work emails* (NOT *control emails*).
- **Phrasal verbs:** *take on* + staff / responsibility (= hire / accept); *step up* + efforts / security (= increase).
- **Phrasal verbs:** *crack down on* + employers / crime (= act strictly against); *hand out* + fines / sentences; *scale back* + service / opening hours / plans (formal).
- **Phrasal verbs:** *switch off* and *burn out* — both intransitive; *burnout* is the noun.
- **Fixed expressions:** *around the clock* (BrE also *round the clock*), *at all hours*, *in the early hours* (with *the*), *fall asleep at the wheel*.
- **Frames:** *Even so, I believe the disadvantages clearly outweigh these gains.* — a clear answer to any "outweigh" question after you list the benefits.
- **Frame:** *Nor is the problem limited to X: …* — inverted negative that widens your point; note the word order (*Nor is*, NOT *Nor the problem is*).
- **Specific example:** France's "right to disconnect" law, in effect since 2017 for larger companies.

## Text 2 — Letter (semi-formal)
> Your new neighbors often make loud noise late at night, and you have not been able to sleep properly for several weeks. Write a letter to your neighbors. In your letter, introduce yourself, explain the problem and how it affects you, and suggest a solution.

Dear Neighbors,

My name is Elena, and I live in Apartment 4, directly below yours. I hope you are settling in well. It was nice to meet you on the stairs last month, and I would like to **build on** that friendly start.

I am writing about the noise from your apartment late at night. For several weeks, music and loud voices have continued well after midnight. I recently **took up** a position as a nurse at the city hospital, so I have to get up at 5:30 a.m. Most nights the noise **keeps** me **up** until one or two, and even then I **lie awake**, **tossing and turning**. As a result, I am **running on empty** at work, where patients **rely on** me to stay alert.

I understand that you want to enjoy your home, so perhaps we could compromise. Would you mind **keeping the noise to a minimum** after 11 p.m. on weeknights? A quick note before parties would also be appreciated.

I would much prefer to sort this out between ourselves rather than **turn to** the building manager, and I would certainly not want to **resort to** filing a noise complaint with the city.

Best regards,
Elena

**Notes**
- **Collocations:** *lie awake* (past: *lay awake*), *keep the noise to a minimum* — a polite, standard phrase for noise complaints (add *the* when you mean specific noise).
- **Phrasal verbs / idioms:** *keep sb up* (= stop sb sleeping: *the noise keeps me up until one*), *toss and turn*, *be running on empty* — describe the effect on you vividly but politely.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *take up a position* (= start a job, formal-neutral); *rely on sb to do sth* (*patients rely on me to stay alert*), NOT *rely to*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *turn to* + person/organization for help; *resort to* + noun / *-ing* (= use as a last option): *resort to filing a complaint*.
- **Phrasal verb:** *build on* + success / a good start — a positive, diplomatic opener.
- **Frames:** *I am writing about …* / *I understand that …, so perhaps we could …* — acknowledge their side before you ask.
- **Frame:** *Would you mind + -ing …?* — the most polite request form; NOT *Would you mind to keep*.
- **Frame:** *I would much prefer to … rather than …, and I would certainly not want to …* — a firm but friendly warning; no contractions in a semi-formal letter to strangers; close with *Best regards,* (AmE *apartment*, BrE *flat*).
