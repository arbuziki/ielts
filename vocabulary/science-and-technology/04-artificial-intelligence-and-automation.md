## Text 1 — Problem and solution
> Artificial intelligence and robots are expected to replace many jobs in the future. What problems might this cause, and how can these problems be solved?

As **artificial intelligence** improves, the most obvious danger is large-scale **job losses**. Firms that **go ahead with** full **automation** can **lay off** hundreds of staff at once, and the first to go are usually those doing **routine tasks**, such as **data entry** or call-center work. Unlike earlier waves of technology, **generative AI** also threatens **white-collar jobs**, so even college graduates may find their skills suddenly out of date. If the gains go mainly to shareholders, AI is likely to **widen inequality** and leave many middle-aged workers **on the scrap heap**, which could in turn fuel social unrest.

The most effective solution is large-scale **reskilling**. Governments should **set up** national **retraining programs** that people can join long before their jobs disappear. Singapore's SkillsFuture initiative, launched in 2015, gives every citizen aged 25 and over credit to spend on approved courses, from coding to nursing, and has helped make **lifelong learning** a normal part of working life. Companies, for their part, could be required to fund training for any workers who **are laid off**. The government should also support the new industries that **take off** as AI spreads, since these will create many of tomorrow's jobs. What governments should not attempt is to **shut down** the technology itself: history suggests that resisting automation fails, whereas preparing people for it works.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *job losses*, *routine tasks*, *white-collar jobs*, *generative AI* — name exactly who is at risk instead of writing *many people will lose jobs*.
- **Topic vocabulary:** *reskilling* (U) and *lifelong learning* (U) — the standard solution pair; no article.
- **Collocations:** *widen inequality*, *retraining programs* — *program* is the natural AmE word for a government plan of this kind (BrE: *scheme*); *be laid off* (BrE: *be made redundant*).
- **Phrasal verbs:** *go ahead with* + automation / a plan; *lay off* + staff / workers (= dismiss because there is no work); noun: *layoffs*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *set up* + a program / a fund / a business; *shut down* + factories / the technology.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *take off* (intransitive) — new industries / a product *take off* = become successful.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *on the scrap heap* — informal; use once, for the human cost.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *Unlike earlier waves of technology, X also…* — shows you understand what is new about a trend.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *Companies, for their part, could be required to…* — *for their part* shares responsibility neatly between actors.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *What governments should not attempt is to…: history suggests that X fails, whereas Y works.* — a strong closing frame for any problem–solution essay.

## Text 2 — Letter (semi-formal)
> Your company has started using new AI software, but many staff do not know how to use it properly. Write a letter to your manager. In your letter
> - describe the situation
> - explain the problems it is causing
> - suggest a training program.

Dear Ms. Clarke,

I am writing about the AI scheduling software that was **brought in** last month. While the system has clear potential, many colleagues are struggling to **get a handle on** it.

The main difficulty is that staff were shown only a short video before the old system was **done away with**. As a result, people face **a steep learning curve** and are **muddling through** on their own. Several colleagues have **ended up** booking appointments by hand, which defeats the purpose of the software, and last week two customers were sent conflicting times. It also **turns out** that few of us understand how the **algorithm** makes its suggestions, so its mistakes are not always noticed. These may only be **growing pains**, but they are already affecting morale and **productivity**.

I would therefore like to suggest a short training program: two half-day workshops run by the supplier, followed by a weekly drop-in session where staff can ask questions. It might also help to appoint an experienced user in each team as a point of contact.

I would be glad to help organize this. Please let me know if you would like to discuss it.

Best regards,

Tom Bennett

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *the algorithm*, *productivity* — show that you understand what the software does and why the manager should care.
- **Collocations:** *a steep learning curve* — always *steep*, never *high*; *face / be on a steep learning curve*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *bring in* + software / legislation / measures (= introduce); *do away with* + the old system (= abolish), often passive.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *muddle through* (intransitive) = manage somehow without proper training or help; *end up* + -ing (= the unplanned result).
- **Phrasal verbs:** *it turns out that* + clause — introduces a discovery politely, without blaming anyone.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *get a handle on* + a new system — ideal for any letter about change at work or school (BrE: *get to grips with*).
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *These may only be growing pains, but…* — softens a complaint while keeping it serious (BrE: *teething problems*).
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *which defeats the purpose of* + noun — a neat way to show that a workaround cancels the benefit.
- **Fixed expressions & frames:** *I would therefore like to suggest… / It might also help to…* — two different suggestion frames for a semi-formal letter; open with *Dear Ms. Clarke,* (period after *Ms.*) and close with *Best regards,* or *Sincerely,*.
