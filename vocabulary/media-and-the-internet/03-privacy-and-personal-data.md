# Privacy & Personal Data
*Media & the Internet · data collection by tech companies, targeted advertising, consent and terms of service, data breaches, the right to be forgotten, government access to data, free services paid for with data*

## Text 1 — Discuss both views and give your opinion
> Technology companies collect huge amounts of personal data about their users. Some people think this is acceptable in exchange for free services, while others see it as a serious threat to privacy. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

In my view, the price of "free" online services is too high.

Technology companies such as Google and Meta offer search engines, email and social networks without charging users anything, and many people believe that collecting **personal data** is a fair price for this. In their view, **targeted advertising** **goes hand in hand with** free access: ads bring in **the lion's share of** these firms' income, and seeing ads for things we actually want is hardly a disaster. Supporters also argue that people who have **nothing to hide** have little to fear.

Critics, however, point out that "free" services **come at a** hidden **cost**. Most users simply **check a box** to accept **the terms and conditions** without reading **the fine print**, so their **consent** is hardly informed. Their **browsing history** and location may be **passed on** to advertisers, and if a company **suffers a data breach**, **sensitive information** can **fall into the wrong hands**.

I side with the critics, because users have no real way of knowing what happens to their data. Regulation can help. The EU's General Data Protection Regulation, which took effect in 2018, gives citizens more control, and in 2023 Meta was fined a record €1.2 billion under it. **It remains to be seen whether** such fines will change companies' behavior, but **it is high time** other countries **introduced** similar laws.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *personal data* and *consent* are U (no *a*, no plural); *the terms and conditions* and *the fine print* both need *the*.
- **Collocations:** *check a box* (BrE: *tick a box*), *suffer a data breach*, *sensitive information* (U) — concrete details that make a privacy argument convincing.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *pass sth on (to sb)* — in the passive: *data is passed on to advertisers* (BrE also: *sold on*).
- **Idioms:** *have nothing to hide* — present it as the opponents' argument; *fall into the wrong hands* — for any leak of data, weapons or money.
- *X goes hand in hand with Y* — two things that come together; reuse: "Economic growth often goes hand in hand with pollution."
- *the lion's share of* + noun — the biggest part; reuse with budget, profits, housework.
- *X comes at a (hidden / high) cost* — points out the downside of something that looks free or easy.
- *It remains to be seen whether* + clause — a cautious, formal prediction for conclusions.
- *It is high time* + subject + past simple — an urgent recommendation (*it is high time governments acted*).

## Text 2 — Letter (formal)
> Even though you unsubscribed from an online store's mailing list, you keep receiving marketing emails and phone calls. Write a letter to the company. In your letter, explain the situation, say why you are concerned about your personal data, and state what you want them to do.

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to complain about the marketing emails and calls I still receive, even though I unsubscribed from your mailing list on March 3.

I **signed up for** your newsletter after buying a coat last winter but later decided to **opt out**. Since then, I have received around twenty emails and three calls to a cell phone number I do not remember giving you.

I am concerned about how my **personal data** is being handled. As my request has been ignored, I cannot help wondering whether my details have been **shared with third parties**. I appreciate that a mailing list is **a means to an end** for any business, but a company that wishes to **meet the needs of** its customers cannot **turn a blind eye to** their stated wishes. As a student who struggles to **make ends meet**, I find the constant pressure to spend money unwelcome.

I would therefore ask you to remove my details from all marketing lists and confirm in writing whether my data has been passed on. You may also wish to **raise awareness of** data protection rules among staff.

I look forward to your prompt reply.

Sincerely,
Daniel Ivanov

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *personal data* — the formal term; avoid *my info* in a formal letter.
- **Collocations:** *share data with third parties* — the precise phrase for data being passed on.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *sign up for* sth / *opt out (of* sth*)* — the two key verbs for subscriptions; both neutral enough for a formal letter.
- *X is (only) a means to an end* — something done to achieve a goal, not for its own sake.
- *meet the needs of* + group — customers, patients, students, seniors.
- *turn a blind eye to* + noun — deliberately ignore; strong but still polite enough for a complaint.
- *struggle to make ends meet* — have barely enough money; reuse in essays on poverty and housing.
- *raise awareness of* + issue — suggests training or a campaign as a solution.
- Register: no contractions; *I would therefore ask you to…*, *I look forward to your prompt reply*, *Sincerely,* after *Dear Sir or Madam,* (BrE: *Yours faithfully*). Dates: *March 3* (BrE: *3 March*).
