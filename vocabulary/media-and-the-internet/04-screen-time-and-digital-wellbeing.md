## Text 1 — Problem-solution essay
> Children spend more and more time looking at screens. What problems does this cause, and what can parents and schools do about it?

Many children now spend most of their free time in front of screens, and in my view this **poses a** genuine **threat to** both their health and their education.

The most obvious problem is physical. Hours of gaming and video-watching encourage **a sedentary lifestyle**, so many children get far less **physical activity** than they need. Screens also **disrupt sleep**: teenagers often **stay up** late chatting online, and **blue light** from their phones makes it harder to fall asleep. This creates **a vicious cycle**, because tired students struggle to concentrate in class and gradually **fall behind** with their schoolwork.

Parents must **take responsibility for** their children's habits. They can **set time limits**, use **parental controls** and, perhaps most importantly, **turn off** their own phones at mealtimes, since children copy what adults do. Schools, meanwhile, should use lessons to **draw attention to** the risks of **excessive use** and **take steps to** keep devices out of the classroom. France, for example, **imposed a ban on** cell phones in elementary and middle schools in 2018, so that recess would be spent talking and playing rather than staring at screens.

If families and schools **address the issue of** **screen time** together, children can enjoy technology **in moderation** without damaging their health.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *screen time* (U, NOT *screen times*); *a sedentary lifestyle* (C, so it needs *a*; verb: *lead*); *blue light* (U).
- **Collocations:** *disrupt sleep*; *excessive use (of* sth*)*; *impose a ban on* + noun — the preposition is ON.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *stay up (late)*, *fall behind (with* sth*)*, *turn sth off* — cause, consequence and solution in three verbs.
- **Idioms:** *a vicious cycle* (BrE: *a vicious circle*) — for any problem that feeds itself; *in moderation* — a natural closing qualifier.
- *X poses a (serious / genuine) threat to Y* — a formal way to state the problem in the introduction.
- *take responsibility for* + noun / -ing — assigns duties in problem–solution essays.
- *draw attention to* + noun — awareness campaigns, lessons, the media.
- *take steps to* + infinitive — always follow it with a real measure, or it sounds empty.
- *address the issue of* + noun — a formal "deal with", good for conclusions.
- AmE school words: *a cell phone*, *elementary / middle school*, *recess* (BrE: *a mobile phone*, *primary / lower secondary school*, *break time*).

## Text 2 — Essay paragraph — quality matters more than hours
> Some people argue that the content and quality of children's screen time matter more than the number of hours they spend online. Write a paragraph supporting this view.

Counting hours misses the point, because what children do on a screen matters far more than how long they spend on it. A teenager who spends an evening learning to code or editing a short film is in a very different position from one who **zones out** in front of endless videos. Minecraft, for instance, has an education edition that teachers use for subjects from math to history, and an hour spent on it can be **time well spent**. Screens themselves are therefore only partly **to blame for** problems such as poor concentration; **passive screen use** is the real issue, and **the jury is still out on** whether creative use does any harm at all. This is why **a blanket ban** would be a mistake: if such a ban **went into effect** in schools, students would **be at risk of** **missing out on** valuable **educational content**. Rather than **putting pressure on** parents to count every minute, experts should help them find **age-appropriate content**. Ultimately, **the responsibility lies with** adults to guide children toward screen use that is creative and social, because the rule should be **quality over quantity**.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *passive screen use* vs creative use — the key distinction for this angle; *educational content* / *age-appropriate content* are U (NOT *contents*).
- **Collocations:** *a blanket ban* — any ban that ignores differences between cases.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *zone out* (informal — use sparingly in essays); *miss out on* + noun.
- **Idioms:** *time well spent*; *the jury is still out on* + noun / *whether*; *quality over quantity*.
- *X is (only partly) to blame for Y* — shares out blame precisely instead of exaggerating.
- *a law / ban goes into effect* — NOT *goes into power*; only for official rules, not family ones. Also: *takes effect*; BrE: *comes into force*.
- *be at risk of* + noun / -ing — any negative consequence.
- *put pressure on* sb *to* + infinitive — parents, governments, companies.
- *The responsibility lies (largely) with* + noun — a formal way to assign duty in a conclusion.
