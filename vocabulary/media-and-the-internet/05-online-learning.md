## Text 1 — Two-part question essay
> Online courses are becoming increasingly popular. Why is this the case, and do you think online learning will ever fully replace face-to-face teaching?

Online courses have become popular for two main reasons. First, they are flexible: learners can **study at their own pace** and fit lessons around work and family. Second, they are cheap or even free. When Harvard and MIT launched the edX platform in 2012, they **set an example** that many universities followed, and today anyone with an internet connection can **enroll in** a **MOOC** taught by leading academics. For people who cannot afford college **tuition**, or who need to **update their skills** while working, this is a strong attraction, and the benefits are felt **across the board**, from retirees to busy parents.

However, I do not believe that online learning will replace classrooms **once and for all**. Having taken an online course myself, I know **firsthand** how easy it is to **put off** assignments when nobody is checking. **Self-discipline** is hard to maintain alone, which partly explains why free courses tend to have **a high dropout rate**. **Sooner or later**, most learners also need a teacher who can answer their questions and from whom they can **receive feedback** in person. For these reasons, I expect **blended learning**, which combines online materials with classroom sessions, to become the norm rather than a fully online system.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *a MOOC* (C), *blended learning*, *self-discipline* and *tuition* (U) — check the article before each one.
- **Collocations:** *study at your own pace*; *enroll in* a course (BrE: *enrol on*); *a high dropout rate*; *receive feedback* (U).
- **Phrasal verbs:** *put sth off* — the standard verb for procrastination; *put off* + -ing.
- *set an example* (for sb) — pair it with a named pioneer, as with edX here.
- *across the board* — = for everyone / in every area; reuse: "price increases across the board".
- *firsthand* — turns personal experience into evidence: *I know firsthand how…*; adjective: *firsthand experience* (BrE usually: *first-hand*).
- *once and for all* — finally and completely: *solve / settle a problem once and for all*.
- *Sooner or later,* + clause — something that is bound to happen.

## Text 2 — Essay paragraph — what classrooms give that screens cannot
> Some people argue that classrooms develop social skills, discipline and teamwork that online learning cannot replace, especially for younger learners. Write a paragraph supporting this view.

For younger learners in particular, a classroom offers far more than lessons. When children work in groups, they learn to listen, take turns and settle disagreements — skills that are almost impossible to **pick up** in front of a webcam. A teacher can also notice when a student is struggling and **go the extra mile** to help, whereas a child **left to their own devices** at home may simply stop working. Schools also provide a daily routine: students must arrive on time, **keep up with** their classmates and meet deadlines, and **in return** they **receive** praise and **feedback** from someone who knows them well. The school closures of 2020 showed what happens without this structure. Many young children found remote lessons hard to follow and fell behind in reading and math, **which in turn** widened the gap between students from richer and poorer homes. Classrooms also bring together children from different backgrounds, **thereby teaching** them to cooperate with people unlike themselves, which benefits society **as a whole**. **The bottom line is that** online tools can support teachers, but they cannot replace **the human touch**.

**Notes**
- **Topic vocabulary:** *the human touch* — always with *the*; a neat closing phrase for any technology-versus-people essay.
- **Collocations:** *receive feedback* (U) — *praise* is U too, so no *a*.
- **Phrasal verbs:** *pick sth up* (skills, languages, habits); *keep up with* + sb / sth.
- **Idioms:** *go the extra mile*; *(be) left to your own devices* — the possessive changes: *their / his / your own devices*.
- *in return (for* sth*)* — what you get for what you give.
- *clause, which in turn* + verb — links a chain of consequences in one sentence.
- *clause, thereby* + -ing — formal "and in this way"; use it once per essay at most.
- *society / the economy as a whole* — widens the argument from individuals to everyone.
- *The bottom line is that* + clause — a neutral, punchy summary; fine in essays, but only once.
