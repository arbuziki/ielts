## Prompt

When we meet someone for the first time, we generally decide very quickly what kind of person we think they are and if we like them or not. Is this a good thing or a bad thing?

## My answer

Trying to keep up with the relentless pace of modern life, people are bound to make quick judgements, including assessing someone's character and whether they are worth our time. While acknowledging the merits of instantaneous judgements, this essay argues that giving people the benefit of the doubt to prove their character over time is far more rewarding in the long term.

First, our instantaneous judment is based on highly subjective factors, such as looks, charisma, and overall attractiveness. Sometimes those qualities can be indicative of a genuinely nice person; however, they just barely touch the surface and can be highly misleading. Not only do those tell nothing about a person's true value, but they can be cunningly utilized by some disingenuous individuals to manufacture an illusion, hiding their true ulterior motives behind it. Indeed, many con artists rely heavily on the deceptive first impression that they make on people.

On the other hand, what snap judgement overlooks is deeply-seated character traits, the bedrock of the particular person, from generosity and reliability to the willingness to lend a helping hand in a difficult situation. Were such qualities manifested instantly, people would make friends instantly; nevertheless, it is advisable to rerfain from quick judgements, as the two following outcomes are equally likely: attract the wrong person and turn down the right person. This uncertainty is highlighted by the labor market: it is HR managers who struggle the most trying to pick up a candidate's problem-solving capabilities, daily work ethic, or collaborative skills behind the facade they hide their true identity behind.

In conclusion, the nature of snap judgement can be extremely deceptive, making it a hit or miss; therefore, a more cautious approach is advisable. Only by giving people a chance to prove their character can we put together the whole picture of a person's real identity.
