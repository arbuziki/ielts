## Prompt

In many places, new homes are needed, but the only space available for building them is in the countryside. Some people believe it is more important to protect the countryside and not build new homes there.
What is your opinion about this?

## My answer

In the modern era, a burgeoning segment of population gravitates toward large city centers. While some claim that this trend bolsters economic growth, at the same time, it exacerbates overpopulation-related issues and puts an immense strain on the rural areas surrounding metropolises. Whether it is necessaty to scale down on residential construction to preserve countryside remains a subject of an intense debate. In this discourse, I will examine the merits and demerits of this phenomenon in an attempt to form my own stance on this matter.

First and foremost, the trend in question is an inevitable consequence of the ongoing globalization seen across many nations. In fact, many seek career and educational opportunities that are prevalent in major cities, which, eventually, draws in a large number of people. Since the building plots are scarce, cities eventually start to sprawl into rural areas. But that being said, those huge hubs are the primary drivers of the economy and it would be counterintuitive to clamp down on their expansion.

On the other side, I believe that there is a wide rage of diverse initiatives available at our disposal to mitigate detrimental impact on rural areas. Namely, rational urban planning such as construction of 4-story residential buildings instead of individual houses optimizes land usage, alleviating the pressure on the countyside. Moreover, I believe this is exactly the issue where government should step in to slow down the migration to major cities. This can be achieved by subsidizing regional cities and fostering equal job opportunities.

To conclude, I understand that the globalization cannot be reversed, which means that this trend will continue to ramp up. Having said that, the adverse effects can be largely mitigated with adequate legislative intervention.
