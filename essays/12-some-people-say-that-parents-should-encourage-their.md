## Prompt

Some people say that parents should encourage their children to take part in organized group activities in their free time. Others say it is important for children to learn how to occupy themselves on their own.
Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

## My answer

In a fast-paced contemporary world, it is becoming increasingly difficult for parents to supervise their children. While in the modern era the notion of "freely roaming children" is rapidly becoming an anachronism due to the abundance of diverse extracurricular programs, there is a burgenoning segment of people claiming that coming out of age requires a certain degree of independence in order for children to become well-rounded, socially fitting adults. Despite the irrefutable advantages of organized activities, I would like to empasize that lumping everyone together is inacceptable, since each kid is unique, and a very nuanced approach is often required.

First and foremost, proponents of organized extracurricular activities for children put forward a compelling argument that unsupervised adolescence often results in juvenile delinquency. Indeed, the lifestyle of uncontrolled teenagers bears little resemblance to docile and complaint behavior every parent aspires for their childred to have. Meanwhile, enrolling kids in some out-of-school activities fosters the development of social skills, collaborative work, and hands-on problem solving. On top of that, such sports activities as summer camps, football, or surfing, to name a few, unprecedentally bolster physical development.

On the other hand, some individuals maintain that organized activities are often accompanied by a certain degree of compulsory. Thus, kids that are forcibly enrolled in an activity that they are not interested in might end up becoming disengaged, which is extremely detrimental for their development. Reflecting on my personal experience, my parents enrolled me in many different programs, but none of them was appealing to me. As a result, I had felt alienated and unfit up until the point when I discovered an activity, that I instantaneously fell in love with.

To summarize, I believe that a more cautious and individual approach is required, since each kid is unique and nuanced. While acknowledging the benefits of controlled activities for some kids, I firmly believe that they can be equally detrimental for others.
