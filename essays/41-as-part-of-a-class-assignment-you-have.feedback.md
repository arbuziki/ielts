**Verdict.** Both questions get relevant reasons and a concrete example (immigration routes for foreign graduates, engineers reading documentation). That puts it at the S10 Band 7 level. The support over-generalizes, and the errors are not rare enough for the S5 2A Band 8. Engnovate's GRA 8 is too high when only half the sentences are error-free and article errors recur.

- **Task Response:** Both parts are addressed. The travel point explains why people *learn* English rather than why they *study in* English abroad. Claims such as "the overwhelming majority of English-speaking countries provides higher standards of living" are sweeping. That is TR 7.
- **Coherence & Cohesion:** There is one paragraph per question. "In light of the above, it is obvious why English…" claims a link that paragraph 2 never set up. Reference is generally sound.
- **Lexical Resource:** "Exert their dominance" and "disposable incomes" are good. "Kill two birds with one stone" is a cliché in a formal essay (trap 1). There are also "leveled at" (for *aimed at*), "an immense traction", and spelling errors (becobe, threir).
- **Grammatical Range & Accuracy:** 5 of 10 sentences are error-free. The errors are articles ("an immense traction", "both English language"), agreement ("the majority… provides"), and a comma splice in the final example list.

**Key corrections**
- "kill two birds with one stone: teach English and boost future career" → "achieve two goals at once: learning English and improving their career prospects"
- "the overwhelming majority of English-speaking countries provides" → "most English-speaking countries provide"
- "immigration programs leveled at those with" → "immigration programs aimed at those with"
- "has gained an immense traction" → "has gained immense traction"
- "both English language and English-based education" → "both the English language and English-medium education"

**To reach Band 8:** Make each paragraph answer its question directly (why study *in* English rather than why learn it). Cut the idioms in favor of precise, plain phrasing.
