## Prompt

WRITING TASK 2

You should spend no more than 40 minutes on this task.

As part of a class assignment you have to write about the following topic.

Some governments say how many children a family can have in their country. They may control the number of children someone has through taxes.

It is sometimes necessary and right for a government to control the population in this way.

Do you agree or disagree?

Give reasons for your answer.

You should write at least 250 words.

## My answer

In the modern era, maintaining optimal fertility rates has become a complex challenge that many nations are grappling with. As overpopulation, one of the most formidable challenges confronting humanity, places an immense strain on societies and infrastructure, some governments resort to imposing additional taxation in the attempt to regulate the population. While acknowledging some demerits of such approach, I firmly believe that the government must step in and take decisive action in order to mitigate the adverse impact of this trend.

First and foremost, in many developing countries, birth rates have skyrocketed over the last decades, which exacerbates perennial issues, ranging from economic deprivation to inaccessible healthcare. As public welfare in many nations struggles to keep up with a rapidly growing population, the government could introduce various legislative initiatives, such as levying higher tax rates on families with many children, thereby discouraging big families, ultimatelly alleviating pressure on social services and infrastructure.

Conversely, some opponents draw attention to a phychological toll on mental well-being of those families that are forced to hold back having more kids that they desire; however, given ongoing economic hardships in some countries, such a legislative deterrent is largely justified. In my opinion, local authorities must raise public awareness about the necessity of such policies: the labor market must adapt to provide jobs for the growing number of younger citizens, otherwise nations might end up with surging unemployment rates, which inevitably results in juvenile delinquency in the long term.

To conclude, I maintain that it is essential to clamp down on skyrocketing birth rates in developing countries, as it is ultimately for the benefit of all citizens. Governments must impose temporary restrictions on how many children a family can have, thereby alleviating pressure on finite resources, while simultaneously transforming local economies to provide job opportunities for a new, larger generation.
