## Prompt

In many cities, planners have separated schools, homes, shops and offices into specific areas which are widely separated from each other. Do you think the advantages of this policy outweigh the disadvantages for city residents?

## My answer

In the modern era, the adverse effects of urban sprawl stemming from the oversights during the planning phase have become a complex challenge that many nations are grappling with. As some developers attempt to counteract the negative impact on local residents by designating distinctive zones within cities, many opponents voice thier concerns, claiming that such an artificial segregation might result in even more detrimental effects. While acknowledging the potential merits of such segregation, I firmly believe that the proposed solution is fundamentally flawed.

First and foremost, separating a city into designated areas according to their purpose, such as residential areas, school areas, or educational institution areas, might exacerbate traffic congestion, since large masses of people will be forced to commute between those districts on a daily basis. On top of that, the general public will be forced to use cars to navigate vast distances between the areas, which ultimately will result in lack of physical activity, thereby placing an immense strain on the healthcare system.

Conversely, proponents of such separation put forward a compelling argument, highlighting the logistical benefits: retail areas are easier to access from the major highways, which drastically reduces heavy truck traffic in the residential areas, thereby alleviating the pressure and improving the quality of life. Moreover, the segregation makes it easier to navigate different parts of the city, which makes planning the itinerary much easier.

To conclude, I maintain that the demerits of such an urban planning initiative massively outweigh the minor benefits: the resulting traffic congestion and the health-related problems will eventually overshadow the minor logistical benefits.
