## Prompt

You should spend about 40 minutes on this task.

Write about the following topic:

Some people think that the teenage years are the happiest times of most people's lives. Others think that adult life brings more happiness, in spite of greater responsibilities.

Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

## My answer

These days, whether the teenage years or the adult life is the happiest time of most people's lives remains a subject of intense debate. Even though I acknowledge the childish nature of this disagreement, I will examine both views and provide some facts from my personal experience.

First and foremost, proponents of childhood being the happiest time put forward poignant exapmles of events that we experience only once in our lifetime: first friendship, first love, first broken heart. Looking back at those memories often makes us emotional and some sensetive individuals even can shed a tear, which makes the argument resonate with a large audience.

But having said that, many people claim that it's the adult life that provides us with independence and financial responsibility, which ultimatelly make us fulfilled and truly happy. That idea appeals to many, since being a mature individual allows us to take the course of our lives in our own hands.

Speaking about my personal experience, I grew up in a small nuclear family since my parents got divorced, and my paternal and maternal sides of the family drifted apart. In conjuntion with the economic deprivation during that time, exacerbated by the geopolitical volatility, my childhood was particularly rife with hardships. At some points, we were barely able to get by and near starvation. But those times are long gone, I grew up, graduated from a university, secured a well-paid role at one of international big tech companies. As an old saying goes, "time heals," and I have an acute awareness of that phenomenon. I still can recall many happy moments of my childhood, but if I looked past that coping mechanism, I would definitely conclude that my adulthood is totally a happier period of my life.

To summarize, I maintain that this is mostly a matter of personal experience rather than a well-established fact. Arguments on bots sides can be equally compelling based on authors' personal experience.
