## Prompt

Some people think it's better to choose friends who always have the same opinions as them. Other people believe it's good to have friends who sometimes disagree with them.
Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

## My answer

In a contemporary world, the old saying about "friends for life" is rapidly becoming an anachronism: the ubiquity of online platforms and social media significantly alleviates the pressure of finding people with similar interests. Having said that, whether people should aim at finding people with the similar opinions in the first place remains a subject of intense debate. While acknowledging the potential merits of such polar bonds, I firmly believe that it is vital to seek people that share our ideas and opinions.

First and foremost, proponents put forward a compelling argument, claiming that friendships between people with similar views last longer, since they eliminate the factor of incessant arguing. On top of that, many studies have shown that social interactions between friends with similar viewpoints positively affect their mental well-being. It goes without saying, that individuals with similar interests tend to gravitate toward each other, forming diverse communities: pet lovers, video game enthusiasts, moviegoers, just to name a few. Reflecting on my personal experience, I am much more comfortable and relaxed among firends with similar takes on our common topics.

Conversely, opponents maintain that bonds between people separated by a vast chasm of their differences prove to be more maturing in terms of self-development. Moreover, such connections teach both individuals to listen intently, find compromises, and put across one's ideas concisely, as this type of friendship is holding on mutual respect.

To conclude, I maintain that friendships between like-minded individuals are overwhelmingly better when considering a vast range of factors from mental well-being to the overall fulfillment of such social interactions.
