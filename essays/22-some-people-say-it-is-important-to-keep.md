## Prompt

Some people say it is important to keep your home and your workplace tidy, with everything organized and in the correct place.
What is your opinion about this?

## My answer

In a fast-paced contemporary world, maintaining an optimal balance between keeping one's workplace in order and chasing life goals has become a complex challenge. While some people claim that a person's neatly curated workplace bears little resemblance to their overall ethic, professional competence, or the essence of their character, others posit that sloppines is often indicative of a person's general lack of care and undermines their productivity. While neither of both lifestyles claims universal superiority, I gravitate toward the habit of keeping a house and workplace well-organized.

First and foremost, I would like to put forward an argument that is hard to refute: neatly organized workplaces and houses usually boost productivity. As our performance is often inextricably intertwined with our ability to perform routine, time-consuming tasks, keeping all the necessary tools at hand helps to cut back on the excessive overhead of sorting out the instruments each time. Needless to say, neatly maintained houses and workplaces bolster our capacity for prolonged, focused work, thereby removing distractions that arise from the constant need to put things to the places where they belong.

Conversely, opponents of this view might appeal to plenty of examples of famous historical figures, such as artists, scientists, or painters that were particularly notorious for their messy workplaces and houses, which did not hold them back from achieving an immense fame and success. Besides this, some individuals claim that the proccess of sorting out one's workplace itself requires a substantial amount of time, which pulls focus away from actuall work.

To conclude, I lean toward the opinion that keeping houses and workplaces is essential for achieving lifetime aspirations, while acknowledging that there are exceptions, and for some this is a matter of personal preference.
