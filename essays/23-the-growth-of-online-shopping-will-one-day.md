## Prompt

The growth of online shopping will one day lead to all shops in towns and cities closing.
Do you agree or disagree?

## My answer

In the modern era, the notion of browsing around local shops is rapidly becoming an anachronism: the overwhelming majority of the population is shopping online. While this trend bestows upon consumers an unprecedented convenience and unparalleled customer experience, there is a burgeoning segment of people emphasizing the adverse long-term impact on local businesses. While acknowledging the potential demerits, I posit that traditional retail will be eventually superseded by online stores, thereby forcing local business either to adapt or become extinct.

An initial point to consider is the root cause of this phenomenon: I believe it stems from the ubiquity of internet services, social media, and online platforms that transform every part of modern life. From that point of view, the decline of offline retail is merely an evolutional step, as this outdated form of commerce is gradually phased out and being replaced with more advanced one. Second, e-commerce offers an incredible user experience: it is possible to instantaneously sift through a vast amount of items, check the availablility, place an order, and schedule a delivery for the most convenient time. As a result, these advantages largely diminish the value of offline retail.

Conversely, opponents of this trend might put forward an appealing argument of supporting small shops as the backbone of local communities. In my opinion, artificially sustaining not viable forms of businesses ultimately might be to the detriment of local residents. Instead of fighting the progress, the discontented could facilitate the transformation of local shops by promoting small businesses on online platforms.

To conclude, I firmly believe that this trend will continue, and in the near future traditional stores will largely disappear, which will mark a significant milestone in the evolution of commerce.
