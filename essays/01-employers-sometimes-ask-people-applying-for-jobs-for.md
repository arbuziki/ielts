## Prompt

Employers sometimes ask people applying for jobs for personal information, such as their hobbies and interests, and whether they are married or single. Some people say that this information may be relevant and useful. Others disagree.
Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

## My answer

Unfortunately, in the contemporary world, it has become widely acceptable for employers to exercise an overreach in one's private life by requesting their personal information when applying for a job. While some claim that getting to know a potential candidate is not harmful in any way, there are some important cases that cannot be overlooked. This discource will examine both sides of this debate.

Some people suggest that this asking personal questions is largely just a part of an initial HR meeting, where both sides share their expectations and aspirations. Simply put, this kind of conversations is nothing more but just an idle chitchat, often put in place to pad out an interview till the technical specialists arrive.

In turn, the opponents maintain that certain personal facts can put job seekers in a disadvantageous position, making whole groups of people susceptible to biased judgement. One clear example of those vulnerable groups is females in their 20s -- by hiring them a company risks to lose a worker do to a pontential maternity leave. It becomes abundantly clear, that an HR manager would opt for a safer candidate. Hence, certain groups of people might hit a glass ceiling when it comes to climbing the career ladder.

To conclude, I have a firm stance on this issue. I think that personal information is not only relevant, but often is used to the detriment of a job seeker. As a matter of fact, it is one of those work and life balance issues, where I think legislative intervention is required. Access to personal details might obstruct the career path for some and should be prohibited by law.
