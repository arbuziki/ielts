## Prompt

Some people say that all levels of education, from primary schools to universities, too much time is spent on learning facts and not enough on practical skills.
Do you agree or disagree?

## My answer

In the modern era, higher education has become relatively accessible for the overwhelming majority of population across many nations. At the same time, some sceptics highlight the surplus of white collar job seekers, claiming that the policymakers should tailor the education system to address the needs of the industrial sector. In this discourse, I will make an attempt to examine merits and demerits of both viewpoints and, hopefully, find a middle ground, since this topic appears to be highly controversial.

First and foremost, the proponents of the industrial-centric model put forward a compelling argument in form of a rule of supply and demand. As things stand, the contemporary world just needs fewer office workers due to pervasive automation and the recent rise of AI. While being a highly-qualified specialist with a science major once was enough to secure a decent job, the latest market shifts drastically reduce the necessety in purely theoretical skills. Moreover, some employers complain that the modern education system merely doesn't prioritize practical skills in education programs, which shifts the strain of providing additional profession-related courses onto employers themselves.

On the other side, I think that purely theoretical jobs are far from becoming an anachronism. As a matter of fact, if we delved into the intricacies of those automation technologies, including AI, we would see an immense effort from highly-qualified workers that made these technologies possible in the first place. On top of that, it is an irrefutable fact that manual work, while being physically demanding, often ends up underpaid, which further exacerbates the shortage of blue collar workers.

To conclude, I don't have a firm stance on this issue. While I acknowledge that the need for intellectual jobs has diminished over the recent years, I also see why many find it unappealing to reconsider their career path in favor of manual work.
