## Prompt

Some people say that now we can see films on our mobile phones or tablets there is no need to go to the cinema. Others say that to be fully enjoyed, films need to be seen in a cinema.
Discuss both view and give your opinion.

## My answer

In the modern era, the notion of watching movies in theatres is rapidly becoming an anachronism: the ubiquity of streaming services, social media, and online video platforms has eliminated the necessity of leaving one's house to enjoy a new blockbuster. As many praise this recent development, highlighting the unprecedented convenience that it bestowes upon a large audience, there is a burgeoning counter-movement of people claiming that the trend diminishes the experience of watching movies on wide screens. While acknowledging the ponential benefits for some, I posit that this digital transformation adversly impacts the whole movie industry on a larger scale.

First and foremost, opponents maintain that the trend is actively destroying the theatrical experience, as watching movies home lacks the unmatched authenticity of theaters. Another point of concern is the reduction of quality of movies that air on streaming services, which is largely attributed to the fact of mass-producing content to fill up online showcases. While those arguments constitue a subjective viewpoint, there is plenty of reports indicating that the revenue in the industry is steadily plummeting, thereby placing an immense strain on movie theather workers.

Conversly, proponets put fowrard a compelling argument: the unparalleled convenience for consumers. Indeed, watching movies on any preferred device at any moment ensures positive customer experience. On top of that, the removal of trips to theaters allows to save on travel expenditures.

To conclude, I believe that the drawbacks of this shift towards streaming services massively outweigh the potential merits. As watching movies on your phone brings immense comfort to consumers, at the same time, it is eclipsed by the reduced quality of the content and the elimination of unique theatrical experience.
