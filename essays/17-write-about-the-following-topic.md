## My answer

In the modern era, the irreversable environmental damage being inflicted by human activity has become an immese disaster that many nations are grappling with. While deeply concerned with this systemic issue, in this essay, I will examine the reasons behind it, and provide suggestions as to what actions governments could take to alleviate the pressure.

First and foremost, the implications of this negative trend have already manifested throughout the world. Indeed, trash islands, floating in the ocean, bear little resemblance to the pristine, ecologically clear world that the futurologists of the mid-20th centrury used to portray. As the adverse effects of this issue had become more prevalent, many studies have undoubtedly concluded the exact root cause: the improper disposal of garbage mostly by developing countries. As things stand, the surging fertility rates, in conjunction with economic deprivation, force govenments to cut corners on "insignificant" areas, one of which, unfortunatelly, is the proper disposal of garbage.

Given the severity of the current situation, I firmly believe that this is exactly the problem where the govenment must step in, and legislative intervention is absolutely required. As a matter of fact, the government could substantially counteract this challenge by intoducing diverse initiatives, ranging from prohibiting the usage of plastics to imposing fines for industries. As many developing countries found it difficult to keep up with the growth of the population, the international organizations must contribute by providing financial support, technologies for waste treatment, and by investing in ecologically clean industries.

To conclude, I am convinced that this is one of the most unprecedented threats that humanity has ever had to face down. The international community must take decisive action in order to clamp down on this threat the sooner the better, or the perilous hazards of this issue will burden many generations to come.
