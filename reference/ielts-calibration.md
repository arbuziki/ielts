## 2. Scoring mechanics

- **Four criteria per task, each a whole band from 0 to 9** [official, S1]. Task 1: TA, CC, LR, GRA. Task 2: TR, CC, LR, GRA.
- **Best-fit rule** [official, S1]: a script has to fully fit the *positive* features of a band to receive it. Features printed in **bold** in S1 are *limiters*: their presence caps the criterion. Examples: GT TA band 4 "not all bullet points are presented", "tone may be inappropriate", "format may be inappropriate". Task 2 CC band 5 "paragraphing may be inadequate or missing". Task 2 TR band 5 main parts "incompletely addressed".
- **Task score** = average of the four criteria, which are weighted equally [official, S3].
- **Writing score**: "Task 2 contributes twice as much as Task 1" [official, S4]. Writing results can be whole or half bands [S5]. The pages I read do **not** publish the rounding rule for combining the two tasks.
- **Overall (four-skill) rounding** [official, S3]: the mean is rounded to the nearest half band. A mean ending in .25 goes up to the half band, and a mean ending in .75 goes up to the next whole band. Examples: 6.25 becomes 6.5, 6.125 becomes 6.0, 3.875 becomes 4.0.
- **[house rule] Reporting format for this project:**
  1. Record the four whole-band criterion scores.
  2. Record the raw task mean (for example 6.75).
  3. Record the task band, rounding the mean to the nearest half with .25 and .75 going up, as in the overall rule (6.75 becomes 7.0, 6.25 becomes 6.5).
  4. Mark any piece whose mean ends in .25 or .75 as **borderline**.
  5. For a combined Writing estimate on paired pieces, use (T1 + 2 × T2) / 3 with the same rounding. Label it as an estimate.
- **Minimum length**: 150 words for Task 1 and 250 words for Task 2. Each criterion is assessed "using a minimum of" that count [S2]. **How short answers are treated:**
  - [official, S4] A short answer may not give enough evidence for the higher bands. [S13] "points will be deducted for overly brief answers". [S14, IDP] a letter under 150 words "will result in a penalty".
  - [official, S1] The descriptors only mention length at the low end. A significantly underlength response can make LR inadequate (band 3). Length may be too short to show control of sentence forms (GRA band 3). An answer of **20 words or fewer is Band 1** on every criterion.
  - [official, S8] A Task 2 essay of about 200 words was scored **5.5**, and the examiner said explicitly that it was under 250 words and needed more development.
  - Over-length is not penalised, but it risks irrelevance and leaves less time [S4].
  - **[house rule]** Count words *excluding* any copied rubric. If a piece is below the minimum, cap TA/TR at **6**. If it is clearly short (about 10% or more under: fewer than 135 words for T1, fewer than 225 for T2), cap TA/TR at **5**. In the score notes, also judge whether there is enough evidence for the LR and GRA bands claimed. These caps are a consistency device and are *not* published IELTS numbers.
- **Copied rubric**: it "must be discounted" [S1, band 1 note]. One official comment states that no credit is given for it [S7, AC 2B Script A]. Copying the prompt also counts against paraphrasing ability in LR [S7, AC 2A Script B].
- **Memorised or plagiarised language** [S1, S2, S4]:
  - Inappropriate memorised or formulaic chunks are a band-4 LR feature.
  - Over-dependence on memorised language is a band-3 feature.
  - **Band 0** applies where there is proof the whole answer was memorised.
  - Partly or wholly plagiarised scripts are penalised. Off-topic or unrelated content is penalised (band 1–2 territory if wholly off-topic).
- **Format** [S2, S4, S5, S12]:
  - Answers must be full, connected text. Bullet points or note form anywhere are penalised.
  - A GT letter needs a suitable opening (salutation) and closing (sign-off). A missing closing lowered TA in two official samples (S5 1B Scripts B and C).
  - Headings or sub-titles inside a letter are an inappropriate format [S12, IDP training material].
- **What GT Task 1 TA checks** [S2]:
  1. The purpose is explained clearly.
  2. All three bullet-pointed requirements are fully addressed.
  3. Each bullet is extended appropriately and relevantly.
  4. The letter format is appropriate.
  5. A suitable tone is used **consistently**.
  The register follows from the recipient: personal, semi-formal or formal [S4]. For semi-formal situations, Cambridge advises staying with formal language because it is polite [S9].
- **Scope clause**: in Task 1, the LR and GRA descriptors for bands 8–9 judge range "within the scope of the task" [S1]. A plain, precise letter can therefore reach the top bands (see the S9 anchor).

## 3. Task 1 (GT letter) descriptors by band, paraphrased from S1

### Task Achievement (GT)
- **9**: Every requirement is met fully and appropriately. Content lapses are extremely rare.
- **8**: All requirements are covered appropriately, relevantly and sufficiently. **All three bullets are clearly presented and appropriately illustrated or extended.** Occasional omissions are allowed.
- **7**: All bullets are covered and clearly highlighted, but **could be extended or illustrated more fully or appropriately**. The purpose is clear and the format is appropriate. The tone is consistent and appropriate, with minimal lapses. Content is relevant and accurate, with a few omissions.
- **6**: All bullets are covered and *adequately* highlighted. The purpose is *generally* clear and there may be **minor inconsistencies in tone**. Some details are irrelevant or inaccurate, some are missing or excessive, and more extension is needed.
- **5**: All bullets are presented, but **one or more is not adequately covered**. The purpose is unclear at times. The tone is variable and sometimes inappropriate. The format is inappropriate in places. Detail is limited, or there is irrelevant material in key areas.
- **4 (limiters)**: Not all bullets are presented. The purpose is unclear or confused. The tone or format is inappropriate.

### Coherence and Cohesion (Task 1)
- **9**: The reader follows the message *effortlessly*. Cohesion very rarely attracts attention and paragraphing is handled skilfully.
- **8**: The reader follows *with ease*. Sequencing is logical and cohesion is well managed, with occasional lapses. Paragraphing is sufficient and appropriate.
- **7**: Organisation is logical with clear progression throughout. A range of devices, **including reference and substitution**, is used flexibly, with some inaccuracy or **some over- or under-use**.
- **6**: The response is generally coherent with clear overall progression. Cohesion is **faulty or mechanical** (misuse, overuse or omission). Weak reference or substitution causes repetition.
- **5**: Organisation is not wholly logical and may lack progression. Sentences are not fluently linked. Devices are limited or overused, and inadequate referencing makes the writing repetitive.

### Lexical Resource (Task 1; Task 2 is the same apart from scope wording)
- **9**: Full flexibility and precision. A wide range is used with very natural, sophisticated control. Minor spelling or word-formation errors are **extremely rare**.
- **8**: A wide resource is used fluently and flexibly for **precise** meaning. Uncommon or idiomatic items are used *skilfully and when appropriate*. There are **occasional** slips in word choice or collocation, and occasional spelling errors with minimal impact.
- **7**: Enough range for *some* flexibility and precision, with *some* less common or idiomatic items. Awareness of style and collocation shows, **though inappropriacies occur**. Only a few spelling or word-formation errors.
- **6**: The vocabulary is generally adequate. Meaning is clear despite restricted range or imprecision. A risk-taker shows wider range with more errors. Some spelling errors, but they do not impede.
- **5**: Limited but minimally adequate. Little variation, frequent lapses in appropriacy, repetition. Spelling errors are noticeable and may cause difficulty.
- **4 (limiter)**: Memorised phrases, formulaic language or wording lifted from the input are used inappropriately.

### Grammatical Range and Accuracy (Task 1 and Task 2)
- **9**: A wide range with full flexibility and control. Grammar and punctuation are appropriate throughout. Minor errors are **extremely rare**.
- **8**: A wide range used flexibly and accurately. **Most sentences are error-free** and punctuation is well managed. Errors are occasional and **non-systematic**.
- **7**: A variety of complex structures with *some* flexibility and accuracy. Control is generally good and **error-free sentences are frequent**. A few errors may persist, so a recurring error type is tolerable here.
- **6**: A mix of simple and complex sentences with limited flexibility. Complex sentences are **less accurate than simple ones**. Errors occur but rarely impede.
- **5**: Limited, repetitive range. Complex sentences are mostly faulty and the best accuracy is on simple sentences. Errors are frequent and may cause difficulty. Punctuation may be faulty.

### Boundary discriminators (Task 1)
| Boundary | TA | CC | LR | GRA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 to 7 | Bullets *clearly highlighted* rather than just "adequately" covered. Purpose clear, tone consistent. | Clear progression *throughout* and flexible reference and substitution, rather than mechanical linking | Some less common items, collocation awareness, few spelling errors | Error-free sentences are *frequent*, and complex sentences are reasonably controlled |
| 7 to 8 | Every bullet *extended or illustrated* with specifics (reasons, examples, details), rather than just stated | Followed "with ease", cohesion well managed, only occasional lapses | *Precise* and *skilful* use of uncommon items, with only occasional collocation slips | *Most* sentences error-free and errors non-systematic, with no recurring article or preposition pattern |
| 8 to 9 | All requirements *fully* met with almost no content lapses | Cohesion invisible and paragraphing skilful | Natural, sophisticated control with spelling errors extremely rare | Errors extremely rare, essentially only slips |

## 4. Task 2 descriptors by band, paraphrased from S1

### Task Response
- **9**: The prompt is explored *in depth*. A clear, fully developed position directly answers every question. Ideas are fully extended and well supported. Lapses are extremely rare.
- **8**: The prompt is addressed appropriately and sufficiently. The position is clear and well developed. Ideas are well extended and supported. Occasional omissions are allowed.
- **7**: The *main parts* are addressed appropriately. The position is clear and developed. Main ideas are extended and supported, **but may over-generalise or lack focus or precision in support**.
- **6**: The main parts are addressed, some more fully than others. The position is relevant, but **conclusions may be unclear, unjustified or repetitive**. Some ideas are under-developed or unclear, and some support is weak or less relevant.
- **5 (limiter)**: The main parts are **incompletely addressed**. Development of the position is not always clear. Ideas are limited and under-developed, possibly with irrelevant detail or repetition. The format may be inappropriate in places.
- **4**: The task is tackled minimally or tangentially. The position is hard to find. Large parts are repetitive.

### CC, LR, GRA (Task 2)
These are as in Task 1, with these paragraphing additions in CC:
- **7**: paragraphing is generally effective, and ideas within paragraphs are generally in logical order.
- **6**: paragraphing is not always logical, and the central topic of a paragraph is not always clear.
- **5 (limiter)**: paragraphing is inadequate or missing.
- **4**: there may be no paragraphing.

LR 9 drops the "within the scope of the task" qualifier and says flexibility and precision are "widely evident". This means a Task 2 essay is expected to show range across the whole essay, not just suit a narrow task.

### Boundary discriminators (Task 2 TR)
- **6 to 7**: All main parts addressed, not lopsided. One position held throughout. Each main idea has a supporting reason or example. A 7 may still generalise somewhat.
- **7 to 8**: Support is specific and relevant, without over-generalisation, and every part of a multi-part question is well extended.
- **8 to 9**: Every part is explored *in depth* and fully extended, with no drift. Even a slightly off-question closing remark is a lapse (see the S5 GT 2B Band 8.5 comment).

## 7. Calibration traps (why AI graders and auto-scorers mis-score)

1. **Rewarding showy vocabulary.** LR 8 requires uncommon and idiomatic items used *"when appropriate"* and *precisely*. Idioms forced into a formal complaint letter, or a cliché in a Task 2 essay, count as *inappropriacies* (LR 7) or as risk-taking with errors (LR 6). The S9 Band 9 letter uses plain words. **Score precision and naturalness, not rarity.**
2. **Over-rewarding templates.** Inappropriate memorised or formulaic chunks are a band-4 LR feature [S1]. Standard formal phrases that fit the register (such as an opening that states the purpose or a closing line about hearing back) are normal: the S9 Band 9 model uses them. Penalise templates when they are generic, don't fit the situation, are in the wrong register, are padded (an all-purpose sentence about being granted something that doesn't match the bullet), or are a pre-learned introduction or conclusion that could sit on any prompt. Do not count template text as evidence of range.
3. **Treating spelling as harmless.** Spelling and word formation are explicitly part of LR at every band. "A few" spelling errors is a band-7 feature. Frequent spelling errors pulled an otherwise adequate essay down to 5.5 (S7 2B Script A).
4. **Giving 9 too easily.** At 9, errors must be "extremely rare" in both LR and GRA, which means isolated slips. Two or three clear grammar errors in about 180 words usually means 8 at best. A recurring error type (articles, prepositions) is **systematic** and rules out 8, because 8 needs non-systematic errors (S5 1B Script D stopped at 7 for exactly this).
5. **Confusing "frequent error-free sentences" with "most error-free".** Band 7 GRA only needs error-free sentences to be *frequent*; band 8 needs the *majority* error-free. Count them (see the rubric).
6. **Accepting bullets that are only stated.** "Covered and clearly highlighted *but could be more fully extended*" is TA 7. "Adequately highlighted" is TA 6. One bullet thin or missing caps TA at 5 or 4 (S5 1A Script B shows a thin third bullet in a Band 7 letter; S5 1B Script B lost a whole bullet and got 5.5 overall). Check *each* bullet for a reason, detail or example.
7. **Mismatched or drifting tone.**
   - Informal letters to friends should not use formal openers or sign-offs.
   - Formal letters should not use contractions, slang or over-familiar closings.
   - Assertive is fine if it stays polite (S5 1B Script C).
   - Mixed register is a common reason letters lose marks [S12]. The tone path is: TA 7 consistent and appropriate; TA 6 minor inconsistencies; TA 5 variable; TA 4 inappropriate.
8. **Ignoring format errors.** A missing closing, missing salutation, headings, bullet points or note form all cost TA or format marks (S5 1B Scripts B and C were marked down for a missing closing).
9. **Ignoring length.** Count words, and exclude any copied rubric before counting. Below 150 or 250 words is officially penalised [S4, S13, S14]. Apply the house caps in section 2. Long is not better: extra content that adds irrelevance can lower TA/TR.
10. **Crediting copied or paraphrased rubric.** No credit is given for it (S7). Heavy echoing of the prompt also signals weak paraphrase (LR).
11. **Rewarding linker density.** CC 7 already notes over- or under-use. Mechanical "Firstly / Moreover / In addition / Furthermore" chains are CC 6 ("mechanical"), and sequencer overuse appeared even in the 7.5 essay (S8). At 9, cohesion "very rarely attracts attention".
12. **Rewarding length or sophistication over answering the question.** For Task 2, check every part of a multi-part question. Lopsided coverage gives TR 6 (S5 2B Script B), and a missing part gives TR 5 or below (S5 2B Script A scored 4). A clear position must run through the whole essay.
13. **Inflating CC when paragraphing is missing.** No effective paragraphing kept a strong letter at 7 (S5 1B Script D). In Task 2, missing paragraphing caps CC at 5.
14. **Halo effect across criteria.** Score each criterion on its own evidence. Official samples routinely split: S5 1B Script B had higher-band LR and CC but its TA dragged the task to 5.5.
15. **Treating anchors as exact.** The same two library letters were rated 6.5 in an older official guide and 6 and 7 in the 2023 PDF (S12 vs S5). Expect ±0.5 disagreement near boundaries, and flag borderline pieces rather than forcing certainty.

## 8. Quick rubric (apply per piece)

```
PIECE: ____  TYPE: T1 letter / T2 essay   REGISTER REQUIRED: formal / semi-formal / informal
WORDS (excl. copied rubric): ___   min 150/250 met? Y/N  -> if N: TA/TR cap 6; >=10% short: cap 5 [house]

TA (T1) - tick all true, take highest band whose positives ALL hold
 [ ] Purpose clear in opening lines                          (<=5 if unclear at times; 4 if confused)
 [ ] Bullet 1 / 2 / 3 each present                           (any missing -> TA <=4)
 [ ] Each bullet extended with specific detail/reason        (all = 8; "could be fuller" = 7; adequate = 6; one thin = 5)
```

## 9. Standardisation rules applied to this project [house rule]

After the first grading pass, four graders had drawn some boundaries differently. A single standardisation pass then applied these thresholds to all pieces:

- **LR spelling and word form:** 0–1 errors places no limit. 2–4 errors ("a few") caps LR at 7. 5 or more errors ("some") gives LR 6, unless range and precision are clearly strong, in which case 7 is the maximum. LR 8 also needs precise, natural, less common vocabulary with only occasional collocation slips. Stock template phrases never count as evidence of range.
- **GRA:** 8 needs at least 70% of sentences error-free, no recurring error type, and a wide range of structures. 7 needs at least 50% error-free and a variety of complex structures; one recurring type, such as articles, is tolerated. 6 applies when fewer than 50% are error-free or complex sentences are often faulty.
- **Letters:** a missing name after the sign-off is not penalised. A salutation or sign-off in the wrong register counts as a tone or format lapse.
- **Task band:** the mean of the four criteria, rounded to the nearest half band with .25 and .75 rounding up. A mean ending in .25 or .75 is flagged as borderline.
